# Varran Ashwick - Threadknife

> [!warning] Private DM Note
> This page contains hidden campaign information, faction material, veinstone experimentation details, and interrogation answers. Do not expose this page to players.

## Summary

**Name:** Varran Ashwick  
**Alias:** Threadknife  
**Name source:** Arkhaven ancestry name CSV, human fantasy-style names  
**Role:** Field cutter, rooftop assassin, cleaner, and interception agent  
**Faction:** The Surveyor Directorate, operating through the deniable black-operations compartment known as the Black Register  
**Operation:** The Anvil Work  
**Current status:** Captured by the party after pursuit through the lane  
**Relevant locations:** [[Morfael]], [[Caer Oryn]], Magpie Lane, the Foundry Quarter  
**Relevant subject:** Veinstone-imbued relic movement and human enhancement trials

Varran Ashwick is the slender, dextrous assassin sent to stop people investigating the movement of veinstone-imbued magical relics through Morfael. They are not a grand villain and should not play like one. They are a trained operative: frightened, injured, angry, professional, and still dangerous.

The sigil designed for the secretive faction is branded into the back of Varran's neck, just below the hairline. The brand is old enough to have healed badly, but the skin around it remains slightly red and irritated. Varran keeps touching their collar whenever anyone looks at it.

---

## Physical Description

Varran is a slender, quick-handed human in their late twenties or early thirties.

They have narrow shoulders, wiry strength, pale eyes, close-cropped dark hair, and a face that looks forgettable by design. Their movements are economical, precise, and predatory. Even bound, they look like they are measuring distances: wrists to blade, boot to throat, wall to rooftop, hand to hidden edge.

They wear layered grey-black street clothes beneath a patched cloak, soft-soled boots, wrist sheaths, and a climbing harness hidden beneath the coat. Their hands are scarred from wire, blades, rope, and alchemical burns.

They carry themselves like someone trained to avoid attention, not command it. They are not broad, imposing, or theatrical. Their danger is in speed, patience, and the willingness to strike first.

---

## The Brand

The faction sigil is branded into the back of Varran's neck.

It marks them as part of a hidden operational cell connected to the Black Register. The brand is also used as an oath mark, a control symbol, and a warning to other agents.

Varran reacts sharply if anyone tries to touch it.

> "Do not touch that."

If pressed:

> "Because some marks are not only for show."

If the party studies it closely, allow them to realise that it may be more than ordinary branding. It itches or burns when Varran lies to certain people, speaks certain names, or breaks compartment rules.

Varran's blunt explanation:

> "So I remember who owns the work."

If questioned further:

> "Cell mark. Oath mark. Warning. Depends who is looking."

---

## Personality At The Table

Varran does not beg immediately. They start with contempt.

They watch whoever is quietest in the group, not whoever is loudest. They answer questions with short lines, insults, and half-truths. They try to work out who is in charge. They look for guilt, hesitation, and rules.

They are injured and breathing hard, but they still smile when they think they have found weakness.

Use Varran as a resisting prisoner, not an exposition machine.

Good opening lines:

> "You people have no idea what you've stepped in."

> "You think this is a smuggling job? It is not."

> "Untie my hands and I will show you how much I know."

> "Ask better questions."

> "I was sent to stop a leak. You are not important enough to be the reason."

## What Varran Knows

Varran knows enough to be dangerous, but not enough to expose the entire conspiracy.

Varran knows the following:

- Veinstone relics are being moved through Morfael toward Caer Oryn.
- Some relics are dead stone. Some still answer when handled.
- The agency is selecting soldiers, prisoners, and desperate volunteers for exposure.
- The work is happening under official protection.
- The orders come through the Surveyor Directorate, but the people running the actual trials are hidden behind compartment names.
- Halwin was involved in moving or identifying a relic.
- Number 17 Magpie Lane was a holding point, not the final destination.
- The next transfer is not in Magpie Lane.
- The next route runs beneath the city approach, through an old service route linked to the drains and foundry works.

Varran does not know the following:

- The ultimate architect of the programme.
- The full purpose of the relics.
- The true nature of the Godscar connection.
- Every location involved.
- The identities of the highest-ranking patrons.

## Core Truths

### The Black Register

The Black Register is a deniable compartment of the Surveyor Directorate. It handles covert work that the public arms of Caer Oryn's government cannot admit to.

Varran's words:

> "The part of the city that admits what the rest of it needs."

If the party does not understand:

> "Spies. Cutters. Watchers. Cleaners. The people who do the work polite men deny ordering."

### The Anvil Work

The Anvil Work is the controlled implantation and exposure programme using veinstone and relic fragments to create enhanced soldiers.

The official justification is defence of the Crown and preparation for threats ordinary soldiers cannot survive.

The actual work is ugly: forced preparation, repeated exposure, failures, deaths, and subjects who are no longer fully stable afterwards.

### The Movement Of Relics

Veinstone-imbued relics are being moved in small quantities, disguised as mundane goods or mineral consignments. Some relics are used as anchor-pieces because they help stabilise raw veinstone during implantation and exposure.

Varran's wording:

> "The relics make the stone behave. That is what I was told. Raw veinstone burns through people. The old pieces shape it. Teach it where to go."

---

## Key Names

### Director Sann Morcant

Director Sann Morcant is the ranking public official whose office shields the operation. He may not personally handle veinstone, but his authority protects the people who do.

Varran should not give this name easily.

Varran's careful answer:

> "Director Sann Morcant signs nothing. That is the point. But his people open doors that stay shut for everyone else."

### Captain Odran Kesh

Captain Odran Kesh is Varran's operational superior in the Black Register. He gives practical field orders.

Varran can reveal this name under pressure before giving up Morcant.

Varran's answer:

> "The one I report to is Captain Odran Kesh. Black Register. Field command. He gives the practical orders. Who gives him his orders? I was not paid to ask."

## Interrogation Answers

### Who are you?

First answer:

> "Someone who should have killed you before you got close enough to ask."

Later:

> "Varran. That is all you get."

---

### Who sent you?

First answer:

> "A man with cleaner gloves than mine."

Later:

> "Captain Odran Kesh. Black Register. If you know the name, you are already in trouble. If you do not, keep it that way."

---

### Who is in charge?

First answer:

> "Nobody you can reach."

If pushed:

> "Orders come through the Surveyor Directorate. Not the clerks. Not the map-men. The quiet side. The side that does not put ink on paper."

If they demand a name:

> "Director Sann Morcant signs nothing. That is the point. But his people open doors that stay shut for everyone else."

Deeper answer under pressure:

> "The one I report to is Captain Odran Kesh. Black Register. Field command. He gives the practical orders. Who gives him his orders? I was not paid to ask."

---

### What are your plans?

First answer:

> "To keep idiots like you from dragging a state matter into the street."

If pushed:

> "The plan is to build soldiers who do not break when the next war comes."

If they ask what kind of soldiers:

> "Faster. Stronger. Harder to frighten. Harder to kill. Men who can hold a gate while ordinary men run from it."

If they mention veinstone:

> "Then you already know enough to die over it."

More honest answer:

> "They are putting veinstone into people. Not just carrying it. Not just studying it. Binding it to bone, blood, and nerve. Most fail. A few do not."

---

### Why are you doing it?

First answer:

> "Because the Crown is surrounded by enemies and cowards."

If challenged:

> "Because walls do not hold forever. Because treaties rot. Because elves smile while counting years we do not have. Because things are waking up in places no patrol wants to stand."

More grounded answer:

> "Caer Oryn wants soldiers that can survive what is coming. That is the reason they give us."

If they ask whether Varran believes that:

> "I believe dead people do not get to argue policy."

If they press morality:

> "Do not look at me like that. You have killed men today for less than a kingdom."

---

### How are you doing it?

First answer:

> "Badly."

Then Varran shuts up.

If pushed:

> "They grind small veinstone fragments down. Mix them with treated blood, salts, and spellwork. Sometimes they use relics as anchors. The body rejects it unless the subject is prepared."

If asked prepared how:

> "Pain. Fever. Starvation. Repeated exposure. Healing magic between attempts. You want the clean answer? There is not one."

If asked about the relics:

> "The relics make the stone behave. That is what I was told. Raw veinstone burns through people. The old pieces shape it. Teach it where to go."

If asked what happens to failures:

> "They die. Or they stop being useful. Depends how sentimental the surgeon is."

---

### Where is it going down?

First answer:

> "Not here."

If pushed:

> "Magpie Lane was a handover point. Nothing more."

If threatened or successfully intimidated:

> "There is a drain at the end of the lane. You saw it. That route runs under the old culvert line. Follow it far enough and it reaches a sealed works entrance below the Foundry Quarter."

More specific:

> "Look for the old hammer mark on the service door. Three bars across an anvil. It opens from inside unless you have a Crown key."

If they ask when:

> "Tonight. After the third foundry bell. That is when the next crate moves."

If they ask what crate:

> "Green-black wrapping. Lead-lined. Two handlers only. If it starts humming, nobody touches it bare-handed."

---

### Are you Crown military?

First answer:

> "No. Worse."

If pushed:

> "Military men salute. We remove problems."

---

### Why attack us?

First answer:

> "Because you were pulling threads out of a state operation."

If they say they were investigating a murder or missing person:

> "Then you were pulling the right thread for the wrong reason."

---

### What happened at 17 Magpie Lane?

First answer:

> "A handover went wrong."

If pushed:

> "Halwin panicked. Someone followed him. Someone bled. The relic was moved before your lot arrived."

If they ask who bled:

> "One of ours. Maybe one of his. There was shouting, then knives, then too much blood for anyone to keep pretending it was controlled."

---

### Where is Halwin?

First answer:

> "Alive when I last heard."

If they press:

> "Taken below. Not by me."

If they threaten:

> "Foundry Quarter. Beneath it. That is all I know."

---

### What was the figurine or relic?

First answer:

> "Not a figurine."

If they ask what it really is:

> "An anchor-piece. Old. Green-black. Ugly little thing. It reacts to veinstone like a tuning fork reacts to sound."

If Nakoa or Teclis pushes the magical angle:

> "It does not cast. It answers. That is the word they use."

## Pressure Points

### Pain Does Not Work Well

Varran is trained for pain. They mock it.

> "You hit like a city guard. That is not praise."

### Fear Works Better

Varran is more vulnerable to the idea that their employers will assume they spoke.

> "You do not understand. If they think I spoke, I am already dead."

### Mercy Works Best

If someone offers protection or a clean handover to a church, a scriptor, or an authority outside the Surveyor Directorate, Varran may give one useful answer.

> "You get me away from Crown hands, and I tell you where the next crate goes."

### The Brand Worries Them

If the party studies the neck brand, Varran becomes defensive.

> "Do not touch that."

If they ask why:

> "Because some marks are not only for show."

---

## What Varran Should Not Reveal Easily

Keep the following locked unless the party does very well:

- The name Director Sann Morcant.
- The phrase The Anvil Work.
- The existence of living test subjects.
- That Halwin may still be alive.
- That the next movement happens tonight after the third foundry bell.

The easiest useful reveal should be:

> "The drain route leads below the Foundry Quarter."

The hardest useful reveal should be:

> "They are putting veinstone into people."

---

## Emergency Escape Attempt

If the interrogation stalls, Varran makes one move.

They twist their thumb out of joint to slip a binding, kick dirt or blood into someone's face, and go for either:

1. a hidden ceramic blade under their boot sole, or
2. the edge of the roof, drain, or alley mouth.

They are not trying to win. They are trying to force the party to kill them or create enough chaos to run.

Line during the attempt:

> "You should have cut my hands off first."

---

## Final Clue To Drop

Before Varran is taken away, killed, or silenced, they should give one hard clue:

> "Third bell. Foundry Quarter. Old service door under the hammer mark. That is where your missing man went. That is where the stone is going. And if you follow it, do not touch anything that hums."

This gives the party a direction, a time pressure, and a warning without dumping the whole conspiracy at once.

---

## Session Use Notes

Varran should fight back verbally. They should not deliver the whole conspiracy unless the party earns it.

Use them to point the group toward the next location, not to explain the entire plot. Their best function is to confirm that the party has found something bigger than a smuggling job and to make the Foundry Quarter feel dangerous before the party gets there.
