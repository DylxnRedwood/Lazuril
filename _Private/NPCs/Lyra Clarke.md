## Lyra Clarke

_Private NPC working page for Lyra Clarke, created as part of Cassian Clarke's private PC development._

Lyra Clarke is the daughter of [Cassian Clarke](../PCs/Cassian%20Clarke.md) and [Rayna Clarke](Rayna%20Clarke.md), and the younger sister of [Leo Clarke](Leo%20Clarke.md). At the beginning of the campaign she is 6 years old.

Lyra is currently staying in [Seabarrow](../../docs/Worldbuilding/Arkhaven%20States/Windmere%20Assembly/Seabarrow.md), in the [Windmere Assembly](../../docs/Worldbuilding/Arkhaven%20States/Windmere%20Assembly.md), with Rayna and Cassian's parents, [Percy Clarke](Percy%20Clarke.md) and [Meri Clarke](Meri%20Clarke.md), while Cassian travels across Arkhaven on pilgrimage.

## Role in Cassian's Backstory

Lyra is one of Cassian's clearest emotional anchors. She is young enough that Cassian's absence may be felt more as confusion, longing, or stories told by adults than as a fully understood duty. Her presence reinforces that Cassian is not simply defending an idea of faith. He is defending the possibility of a safe future for his children.

Her name also naturally echoes Cassian and Rayna's celestial themes. Whether that name is coincidental, chosen for a constellation, or given because of its musical association with the heavens can be decided later.

## Personality Direction

Lyra is intentionally open for future development. As a starting point, she may be imaginative, affectionate, and prone to absorbing the moods of adults around her. She might remember Cassian through simple things: the locket, bedtime stories about stars, moonlit walks, or being shown how to find a familiar constellation.

If used in correspondence, her voice should be simple and emotionally direct rather than exposition-heavy.

## DM Use

Lyra can be used to keep Cassian's story grounded. The more cosmic his arc becomes, the more important it is that his reason for caring remains personal.

She may also be useful for subtle celestial foreshadowing. A child's drawing of a dark ring around the sun, an innocent comment about the moon looking wrong, or a nightmare described in childish terms can carry more weight than formal exposition.

As with Leo, Lyra should be handled carefully. She is a stake and a source of emotional truth, not a cheap threat.
