## Cassian Clarke

_Private working page for Cassian Clarke. This page is intended for campaign development, DM reference, future session notes, private continuity tracking, and the long-term celestial mystery tied to the Godscar and the weakening of the old god's prison._

Cassian Clarke is a Human Paladin of the Oath of the Watchers, devoted to Selune through the celestial order of moon, stars, sunlight, navigation, and sacred vigilance. At the beginning of the campaign, he is 41 years old. He is an acolyte, husband, father, scholar, pilgrim, and protector whose faith is rooted not only in worship, but in observation.

Cassian comes from [Seabarrow](../../docs/Worldbuilding/Arkhaven%20States/Windmere%20Assembly/Seabarrow.md), a coastal town in the [Windmere Assembly](../../docs/Worldbuilding/Arkhaven%20States/Windmere%20Assembly.md). His life has been shaped by sea winds, starlight, family duty, religious tolerance, and the difficult belief that truth can be approached through both prayer and measurement. His defining phrase is simple: **There are two paths to the truth, religion or science. I choose to take both.**

Cassian's personal story begins as a Selunite pilgrimage, but privately it is one of the earliest player-facing routes into the old god arc. He believes he is investigating the fading of Selune's worship and the spread of religious conflict around celestial sites. In truth, his studies of the stars, the moon, the sun, and the accretion disk are bringing him close to the mechanism that keeps the old god sealed beneath the [Godscar](../../docs/Worldbuilding/Arkhaven%20Lore/The%20Godscar.md).

## Core Identity

Cassian is not a zealot. His faith is sincere, but it is not narrow. He grew up among sailors, healers, worshippers, doubters, priests, and practical people who understood that a prayer before voyage and a correct reading of the stars could both save a life.

His devotion to Selune is therefore not only about moonlight, comfort, and guidance. It is about the idea that light can reveal a path through darkness without destroying the mystery of the dark itself. Cassian reveres the heavens because they are beautiful, but he studies them because beauty alone is not enough. If the stars guide ships, then their movement matters. If the moon governs tides and rites, then its changes matter. If the sun is circled by an accretion disk that no scholar can fully explain, then that too must matter.

His Oath of the Watchers should be understood through this lens. Cassian watches thresholds: the line between faith and knowledge, light and darkness, the mortal world and the celestial, the safety of home and the vastness beyond it. He does not yet understand how literal that calling may become.

## Family and Origin

Cassian was born and raised in Seabarrow, in the Windmere Assembly, the son of [Percy Clarke](../NPCs/Percy%20Clarke.md) and [Meri Clarke](../NPCs/Meri%20Clarke.md). Percy is an elderly sailor whose life on the water taught Cassian the value of discipline, navigation, weather sense, and reading the sky when there is nothing else to trust. Meri is a herbalist whose skill, patience, and service to the local community gave Cassian his first understanding of care, first aid, and practical compassion.

Both of Cassian's parents worshipped Selune and were deeply involved in the religious and social life of Seabarrow. They were not exclusionary about it. Seabarrow was a tight-knit coastal community where many gods were honoured, especially those connected to the sea, stars, tides, travel, healing, and safe return. Different households kept different customs, but open contempt for another person's sincere worship was seen as ugly, dangerous, and bad for the life of the town.

This upbringing matters because Cassian's faith is naturally pluralistic. He can oppose Sharite violence without believing every follower of another god is an enemy. He can argue with religious doctrine without losing respect for devotion itself. He is more likely to ask why someone believes something before condemning them for believing it.

## The Sea, the Stars, and the Sun

Cassian's love of astronomy came from two places at once.

From his parents' devotion to Selune, he inherited reverence for the moon, night, silver light, holy signs, and the comfort of celestial guidance. From Percy, he inherited the practical sailor's relationship with the sky. Stars were not simply distant lights. They were routes, bearings, warnings, seasonal markers, and promises that even a ship lost in darkness could find its way home.

As a young man, Cassian became familiar with constellations, star lore, lunar rites, tidal associations, and the religious meanings attached to the night sky. Over time, however, his interest moved beyond inherited meaning. He wanted to know what the stars were doing, not only what people said they represented.

That curiosity eventually widened to the sun. Cassian became fascinated by the strange accretion disk that surrounds or appears to surround Lazuril's sun. He does not know whether it is natural, magical, divine, artificial, or some mixture of all four. He is especially interested in whether the disk changes the quality of sunlight that reaches Lazuril, and whether those changes have any measurable effect on moonlight, celestial rites, magic, navigation, dreams, crops, tides, or the behaviour of divine power.

This makes Cassian an unusually useful character for the campaign's deeper cosmology. He is not simply looking up at stars. He is asking why the world sees the sun through a filter.

## Studies in Gullspire

Cassian eventually travelled to a parish in the mountains of [Gullspire](../../docs/Worldbuilding/Arkhaven%20States/Havenori%20Republic/Gullspire.md), in the south east of the [Havenori Republic](../../docs/Worldbuilding/Arkhaven%20States/Havenori%20Republic.md), to pursue religious and celestial studies. The mountain air, clear night skies, and reduced light made the region valuable to Selunite observers, star charters, navigators, and scholars of the heavens.

Gullspire was not an isolated human monastery. The surrounding area was shared with dwarves and many other peoples drawn by its high passes, stone roads, old shrines, observatories, and places of learning. This gave Cassian a broader view of faith and science than he might have developed in Seabarrow alone. The sky was one subject, but different peoples approached it through different languages, instruments, rites, and inherited fears.

It was in Gullspire that Cassian met [Rayna Clarke](../NPCs/Rayna%20Clarke.md), a star charter. Rayna's work combined observation, mapping, mathematics, navigation, and religious meaning. Where Cassian often saw questions, Rayna saw patterns. Their relationship grew out of shared nights beneath clear mountain skies, arguments over interpretation, and the strange intimacy of two people looking at the same heavens and realising they were both trying to understand what they loved.

Cassian and Rayna married and had two children, [Leo Clarke](../NPCs/Leo%20Clarke.md) and [Lyra Clarke](../NPCs/Lyra%20Clarke.md).

## The Sharite Conflict

The peace of Gullspire did not last.

The region came under increasing pressure from devotees of [Shar](../../docs/Worldbuilding/Religions/Shar.md), who saw Selunite observatories, moonlit rites, star ledgers, and celestial studies as violations of sacred darkness. To these Sharites, the night was holy because it concealed. The stars were not guides, but wounds in the dark. The moon was not a blessing, but an intrusion. The sun's accretion disk was not a phenomenon to be measured, but a veil that mortals had no right to lift.

At first, Cassian believed the attacks were territorial and religious in the ordinary sense. Rival devotees wanted control of high places, observatories, archives, and mountain routes. Yet the pattern soon became stranger. The Sharites did not only attack shrines. They burned star charts, stole solar observations, sabotaged instruments, threatened star charters, and tried to silence those studying the accretion disk.

The local Sharite faction should currently be treated as a visible antagonist group rather than a fully understood cult. For working purposes, they may be referred to privately as **The Veil of the New Moon**, an orthodox Sharite movement that believes celestial study is a desecration of holy concealment. They are not automatically old god worshippers. Their surface-level doctrine is coherent within Sharite thought: some truths are dangerous because exposure itself can wound the world.

That distinction is important. Cassian should oppose their violence and fanaticism, but later revelations may show that some of their fears were not entirely baseless.

## Learning to Fight

Cassian did not begin as a warrior. He knew practical first aid from Meri, discipline and endurance from Percy, and enough physical resilience from life in coastal and mountain communities to protect himself in ordinary danger. But the attacks around Gullspire changed what he believed was required of him.

With Rayna and their young children living in a region increasingly threatened by religious violence, Cassian began to train in combat. At first, this was practical self-defence. He wanted to protect his family, his parish, and the scholars who were being targeted. Over time, that training became something deeper.

His faith hardened into vigilance. His scholarship became duty. His instinct to protect his home became an oath.

As a Watchers Paladin, Cassian should not be played as a crusader looking for heretics to punish. His oath is about guarding against intrusion, corruption, unseen threats, and powers that stand outside ordinary mortal understanding. He is a father who learned to fight because the people he loved were no longer safe. That should remain central to his tone.

## The Pilgrimage

Eventually, the fighting around Gullspire calmed. That should not be mistaken for resolution. From Cassian's perspective, the violence receded before the questions were answered.

Word reached the parish that similar tensions were appearing across [Arkhaven](../../docs/Worldbuilding/Arkhaven.md). Selunite communities were shrinking. Some shrines were being abandoned. Sailors reported uncertainty in old star paths. Priests claimed that certain rites felt weaker. Scholars whispered that the accretion disk was behaving strangely, though few agreed on whether this was astronomy, magic, omen, or madness.

The parish tasked Cassian with travelling across Arkhaven as an acolyte and investigator. His public task is to learn why fewer people seem to be worshipping Selune, why conflict around celestial sites is spreading, and whether there is a way to resolve or lessen that conflict before it reaches places that have so far remained safe.

Cassian and Rayna agreed that he should go, though the decision was painful. For safety, Rayna took Leo and Lyra back to Seabarrow, where they could live with Percy and Meri in the Windmere Basin while Cassian began his pilgrimage.

## The Locket

Cassian carries a locket from Rayna. In the right light, its surface reflects the pattern of the stars from the night they first met. Hidden within it is a small mechanism that opens to reveal images of Rayna, Leo, and Lyra.

To most people, the locket is sentimental. To Cassian, it is a private anchor. It reminds him that the heavens are not abstract. Stars are how he met his wife. The moon watched over his vows. The sky helped shape the life he is now trying to protect.

The locket can also become a campaign object later. Its reflected star pattern may match an older chart, a rare alignment, a Selunite warning, a Sharite taboo, or a forgotten record of the celestial lock. This should not be revealed early unless needed. For now, it is a keepsake with the potential to become a key.

## Current Goals

Cassian's immediate goal is to understand why Selune's worship appears to be declining and why Selunite communities are being targeted or unsettled across Arkhaven.

His personal goal is to protect Rayna, Leo, Lyra, Percy, Meri, and the faith tradition that shaped his life.

His intellectual goal is to understand the heavens, especially the relationship between the stars, the moon, the sun, and the accretion disk. He wants to know whether the disk is magical, physical, divine, artificial, or something older than those categories.

His spiritual challenge is to learn whether light always reveals truth, or whether some forms of light can also expose the world to danger.

## DM-Only: The Celestial Lock

This section is private to the DM and must not be made player-facing too early.

The accretion disk around Lazuril's sun is not a natural astronomical feature. It is the celestial lock and filter of the old god's tomb beneath the Godscar. When the old god came to Lazuril, it did so because the world's sunlight carried a desirable strength, quality, or hidden radiance that allowed it to root itself into the world and corrupt life, thought, dream, faith, and possibly magic.

The old god was not fleeing the sun. It was seeking a particular kind of light.

When the old god was defeated and sealed beneath the Godscar, the prison could not rely on stone alone. The body or core of the old god was sealed below, while its nourishment was cut off above. The accretion disk was created or imposed around the sun to alter the sunlight reaching Lazuril. It does not extinguish the sun, because that would destroy the world. Instead, it filters, bends, weakens, or changes the light so the old god cannot receive full exposure to the radiance it once fed upon.

This means the disk is both a lock and a mercy. It prevents the old god from fully waking, but it also means Lazuril has never truly seen its own sun unfiltered since the sealing.

As the old god's tomb weakens, the disk begins to destabilise. Dark gaps, warped arcs, irregular flares, and changed light quality are signs that more unfiltered sunlight is reaching Lazuril. Those moments strengthen the old god's influence. The effect may appear as harsher light, wrong shadows, failed moon rites, corrupted dreams, strange plant growth, veinstone responses, altered tides, or moonlight that feels polluted rather than absent.

Cassian's studies should slowly lead him towards this truth, but the early campaign should frame it as astronomy, religious decline, and Sharite conflict rather than cosmic imprisonment.

## DM-Only: Sharites and the Old God

The Sharites around Gullspire should be divided beneath the surface.

The orthodox Sharites of the Veil of the New Moon oppose Selunite celestial study because they believe the night is sacred concealment and that some truths should remain hidden. They are violent, secretive, and dangerous, but they are not necessarily servants of the old god.

A corrupted splinter within or near them has begun to interpret breaks in the accretion disk as sacred openings. They believe they are receiving deeper revelations from Shar. In truth, the old god is exploiting their prayers, dreams, and fear of light. They may speak of the Dark Behind the Dark, the Starless Voice, or a silence older than night. These names should appear slowly and inconsistently before the true connection is revealed.

This allows the Sharite conflict to remain morally and politically complicated. Cassian is right to oppose their attacks. The orthodox Sharites may still be wrong in method but partly right that some celestial knowledge is dangerous. The corrupted splinter is actively useful to the old god, whether or not its members understand what they serve.

## Campaign Use

Cassian works best when his scenes involve guidance, uncertainty, corrupted omens, family pressure, conflicting religious interpretations, and evidence that the heavens are measurable but not simple.

He should receive clues in layers. Early clues can be star charts that no longer match, sailors reporting wrong bearings, Selunite rites that feel diminished, Rayna's notes on solar irregularities, and rumours of Sharites burning observatory records. Later clues can include veinstone reacting to dawn light, moonlight carrying strange effects after solar disk anomalies, dreams shared by people who saw the same dark arc in the sun's disk, and old texts suggesting that the disk is not around the sun by accident.

Cassian's greatest pressure point is his belief that truth can be found by bringing things into the light. The campaign should eventually test whether he can accept that light itself may have been the danger the world was protected from.

## Open Questions

What exactly did Rayna notice about the accretion disk before Cassian left Gullspire?

Did the Sharites attack Gullspire because of general Selunite hatred, or because Rayna and the star charters had already charted something dangerous?

Does Cassian's locket only show the night he met Rayna, or does that star pattern have older significance?

How much does the parish at Gullspire know about the solar disk, and how much has it deliberately withheld from Cassian?

If the accretion disk is weakening, is Cassian meant to restore it, understand it, or decide whether the world has the right to see the sun as it truly is?
