The Lanternwise¶

Description¶

The Lanternwise are a loose network of informants, travellers, singers, scribes, scouts, old soldiers, river pilots, teachers, and ordinary people who make a habit of knowing things they probably should not.

On the surface, they are harmless enough. They carry stories, news, warnings, letters, rumours, songs, and small acts of charity across Arkhaven. In truth, they are a quiet resistance against tyrants, false saints, slavers, cults, corrupt guilds, and rulers who think truth belongs only to them.

They are not a formal rebellion. They do not hold land or march under one banner. Most members only know a handful of others, which is exactly the point.

They are most active around the major roads, ports, crossings, and places where information is controlled, especially near The Godscar, the Crimson Chain, The Vermillion Crown, The Leridian Concord, Republic of Havenor, and the archive-towns watched by the Court of Thalóriel.

Purpose¶

The Lanternwise exist to keep people, stories, and warnings moving.

They hide witnesses, move refugees, copy records before they vanish, expose false miracles, and make sure powerful people cannot simply erase what happened.

To them, a burned village, a missing convoy, a forged confession, or a murdered scribe is not just another local tragedy. It is evidence.

Rules¶

The Lanternwise do not rule in their own name. They may interfere, expose, rescue, smuggle, or sabotage, but they do not crown kings or seize towns.

They do not kill for convenience. Violence is used when there is no cleaner way to stop something worse.

They preserve true accounts, even ugly ones. Names, maps, letters, songs, ledgers, and witness statements matter.

They work in small cells. No member should know enough to bring the whole network down.

They do not sell out the helpless. A person under Lanternwise protection is not a bargaining chip.

Personality¶

The Lanternwise are warm, nosy, stubborn, and difficult to pin down.

They are the sort of people who ask too many questions, remember too many names, and turn up at exactly the wrong time for whoever is trying to keep something quiet.

They prefer quiet interference over grand speeches. A good Lanternwise victory is usually one nobody notices until the prisoner is gone, the second copy survives, or the truth is already being sung three towns away.
