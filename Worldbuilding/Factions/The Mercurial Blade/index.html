The Mercurial Blade¶

Description¶

The Mercurial Blade is a private order of mages, spies, duelists, illusionists, diviners, magical scholars, and agents who believe magic is too dangerous to be left entirely in the hands of kings, priests, armies, or universities.

They are not a formal academy, though many of them were trained in one. They are not a simple spy network either, though they are very good at behaving like one.

On the surface, the Mercurial Blade appears as patrons, tutors, court mages, duel instructors, translators, negotiators, and arcane consultants. Beneath that, they trade in magical knowledge, coded correspondence, discreet interventions, stolen research, and quiet corrections when powerful people become careless with spellcraft.

They are most often found in cities where magic and politics overlap, especially around The Leridian Concord, The Vermillion Crown, Court of Thalóriel, and the larger ports of Republic of Havenor.

Purpose¶

The Mercurial Blade exists to keep dangerous knowledge moving, but not freely.

They preserve spells, expose reckless magical research, steal dangerous texts before worse people can use them, and quietly remove mages who become a public problem.

Their belief is simple enough. Magic should not belong to one throne.

The problem is that they often believe it should belong to them instead.

Rules¶

No ruler owns a school of magic.

A dangerous spell should be recorded before it is used.

Magical disasters are to be contained before blame is assigned.

Never reveal serious arcane knowledge to someone too ambitious, desperate, or stupid to survive it.

A mage who hides catastrophic research becomes part of the problem.

Subtlety first. Duels second. Fireballs last.

Personality¶

The Mercurial Blade are clever, sharp, arrogant, and difficult to trust completely.

They like masks, coded letters, clean blades, hidden spellwork, and conversations where every sentence has three meanings.

They are useful allies when the problem is magical, political, or both.

They are dangerous enemies because they rarely look like enemies until the spell is already finished.
