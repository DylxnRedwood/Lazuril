Free Road Alliance¶

Description¶

The Free Road Alliance is a loose agreement between towns, road wardens, caravan houses, guild councils, minor lords, independent ports, bridge-keepers, and settlements that are tired of being crushed between stronger powers.

They are not a kingdom. They are not an army in the proper sense. They are a promise that certain roads, markets, bridges, ferries, and border towns should stay open, even when rulers would rather close them.

The Alliance is strongest along trade routes, frontier roads, Godscar crossings, and places where local people would otherwise be swallowed by the ambitions of larger states. They are common around the Crimson Chain, the Thornbelt, Republic of Havenor, The Vermillion Crown, The Leridian Concord, and smaller settlements that need neutrality to survive.

It sounds noble. Sometimes it is. Other times it is just a lot of frightened towns agreeing that being useful is safer than being conquered.

Purpose¶

The Free Road Alliance exists to keep movement possible across Arkhaven.

They protect neutral roads, escort caravans, ransom travellers, maintain bridges, settle toll disputes, and try to stop local conflicts turning into full annexations.

A village under the Alliance seal is not always safe, but it is harder to erase quietly.

Rules¶

An Alliance road is neutral unless an army breaks that neutrality first.

A marked town must shelter lawful travellers, wounded envoys, and registered caravans when it can.

Banditry, false tolls, hostage-taking, bridge-burning, and forged road charters are treated as crimes against all members.

The Alliance does not overthrow states. It survives by being useful enough that even powerful rulers hesitate before crushing it.

Members may argue, bribe, stall, and scheme, but they do not betray the road itself.

Personality¶

The Free Road Alliance is stubborn, practical, bureaucratic, and constantly arguing with itself.

Its people like ledgers, seals, road markers, escort contracts, witness stones, and emergency councils that go on far too long.

They are not romantic heroes. They are mayors, wardens, merchants, ferrymen, clerks, and tired soldiers trying to keep the next bridge standing.

When they work, nobody notices. When they fail, towns burn and the roads close.
