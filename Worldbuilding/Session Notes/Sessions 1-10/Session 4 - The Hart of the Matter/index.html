Session 4: The Hart of the Matter¶

Played: 13 July 2026
Location: Morfael to the High Quarter of Caer Oryn
Starting point: Chapel of the Last Lantern, Morfael

Back to Caer Oryn¶

The party left Morfael and travelled directly back to Caer Oryn with a clear destination and a plan already forming.

The Gilded Hart Hotel.

Room 13.

It was the location Garron Duskholt had given them during his interrogation, and the same room identified in the courier note connected to the missing lizard figurine. Whatever the Obsidian Register had arranged there, the party intended to find it.

After entering the High Quarter, they passed through wide, immaculate streets surrounded by fantastical stores, expensive eateries, private residences and sprawling noble manors. The district was a world apart from the crowded lanes of Morfael and the smoke-stained streets of the Foundry Quarter. Everything appeared carefully maintained, from the pale paving stones beneath their feet to the brass lamps and clipped trees lining the roads.

The party stood out, but coin and confidence carried them onward.

The Gilded Hart¶

The Gilded Hart occupied a prominent position on Hartglass Row, its cream-stone frontage illuminated beneath a polished brass-and-glass canopy. Elegantly dressed doormen welcomed the party at the entrance before directing them into the warm, busy reception hall.

Marcelline Orras, the hotel’s immaculate proprietor, kept a watchful eye over the establishment while Selda Morn worked behind the reception desk.

The party asked about room 13.

Unfortunately, it had already been booked.

Rather than draw attention by pressing the matter, they arranged rooms of their own nearby. Teclis and Alekthar took the superior room numbered 14, while Nakoa and Cassian booked the standard double room numbered 12.

They were now positioned on either side of their target.

Dinner with a View¶

Their stay included a complimentary fine-dining experience, and the party were soon seated together beside one of the dining room’s tall windows.

The meal offered a taste of High Quarter excess. Fresh bread, carefully prepared meats, rich sauces, fine accompaniments and plentiful alcohol were brought to the table by attentive staff. For a brief time, the party were able to eat and drink among the city’s wealthy patrons while appearing to be ordinary guests.

That pretence did not last long.

From their seats beside the window, they noticed a sudden rush of Civic Directorate employees moving along Hartglass Row. The officials were heading in the direction of House Vaelisar.

The movement was too hurried and too coordinated to be routine.

The Maid Who Survived¶

The party politely questioned one of the waiters about the Directorate activity and whether he had heard any rumours concerning House Vaelisar.

The waiter was reluctant at first, but admitted that he knew a young woman named Resha who had worked as a maid within the Vaelisar household at the time of the incident.

Resha had been inside the manor when everything went wrong.

Unlike most of the household, she had escaped alive, although she had suffered a severe blow to the head during whatever had occurred within the house. Since losing her position, Resha could often be found at Starflux, a coffee shop situated on the corner of Hartglass Row.

The news changed the nature of the party’s investigation. House Vaelisar was no longer merely a sealed ruin filled with second-hand rumours and reports of body bags.

There was a living witness.

Alekthar Scouts the Manor¶

During the meal, Alekthar decided to investigate the sudden Directorate movement for himself.

Leaving the others at the hotel, he made his way towards House Vaelisar. From a distance, he could see two pike-wielding guards stationed before the front gates. Beyond them stood the manor itself, visibly broken and shattered. Windows had been blown out, sections of the structure had collapsed, and the building showed every sign of having suffered a violent and deeply distressing event.

More guards paced within the walls, patrolling the outer grounds surrounding the manor.

Alekthar approached the gate and questioned the two men stationed there.

What had happened?

Why had the Civic Directorate placed the manor under lockdown?

Most importantly, who had ordered the property sealed?

The guards remained tight-lipped and offered little of substance. Nevertheless, the conversation gave Alekthar enough time to inspect the entrance closely, study the surrounding grounds and gain a better understanding of the number and placement of the men inside.

Once he had learned all he could, Alekthar turned to leave.

One of the guards called him back.

Roderick Valeborne.

Valeborne was the man overseeing the sealing of House Vaelisar. Should the party obtain information regarding the incident, or wish to pursue the matter through official channels, he was the man they would need to speak with.

Rest and Observation¶

Following the meal, the party retired to their rooms.

They entered separately to avoid drawing unnecessary attention and took the opportunity to rest fully, enjoying comforts rarely available during their travels.

The superior room occupied by Teclis and Alekthar was particularly impressive. It was spacious, warmly furnished and opened onto a large balcony overlooking Hartglass Row. From there, they had a clear view across the surrounding streets and towards the damaged Vaelisar manor.

It was an excellent vantage point.

Due to his elven trance, Teclis completed his rest before the others. He made his way downstairs with the intention of visiting Starflux and locating Resha.

Before leaving the hotel, however, he overheard a conversation between a young man dressed in brown leathers and an older gentleman whose bearing immediately suggested a long military career. The older man spoke with the confidence of someone who had survived more than one war.

“Them men behind the gate, they ain’t no soldiers. The way they walk, I can tell. The footwork and pacing isn’t right.”

Teclis took note.

The men guarding House Vaelisar might have been dressed and equipped like soldiers, but according to someone experienced enough to recognise the difference, they were merely pretending.

Whatever their true purpose, it was not the ordinary protection of a damaged building.

Starflux After Hours¶

Teclis continued along Hartglass Row until he reached Starflux.

Unfortunately, the coffee shop was closed.

Teclis, however, had never been particularly accepting of the word “no”.

With a click of his fingers, he conjured a large mechanical music box in the middle of the quieting street. The device immediately began blaring out a fine tune, filling Hartglass Row with music that was entirely too loud and far too cheerful for the hour.

Teclis waited.

It did not take long.

A sash window above the coffee shop was thrown open and a woman leaned out. She was not Resha, but the owner and principal barista of Starflux, now roused from her evening by an elf and his magically summoned street orchestra.

Teclis apologised for the unusual method of gaining her attention before asking whether she knew Resha.

“Of course I know her. Fine young girl. Poor thing lost her job with the trouble at the Vaelisar manor. That’s why I’m advertising for an assistant.”

Teclis glanced towards the shopfront and noticed a newly displayed advertisement seeking a barista.

The owner explained that the law required her to advertise the position publicly rather than simply giving it to the person she wanted. Nevertheless, the outcome had apparently already been decided.

Resha would be arriving the following morning for her first shift.

After a brief exchange concerning Resha, the closed shop and the music still echoing along the street, the woman withdrew into the building.

As she slammed the sash window shut, Teclis heard her mutter:

“Pompous prick.”

The Plan for Room 13¶

Meanwhile, the rest of the party had begun scheming.

Alekthar cast detect magic from room 14, searching for any obvious magical presence beyond the walls that might reveal where the relic had been hidden.

Nakoa and Cassian listened closely from room 12, attempting to detect movement within the room between them.

They heard nothing.

There appeared to be no sign of life within room 13.

The coast seemed clear.

Cassian positioned himself in the corridor to keep watch. Alekthar moved onto the balcony, from where he could observe the neighbouring room and the street below. Nakoa approached the door to room 13 and began working on the lock.

By the time Teclis returned from Starflux and made his way upstairs, Nakoa was already preparing to enter. Teclis joined Cassian in the corridor while Alekthar leaned farther over the balcony, guiding Nakoa from outside as he moved through the room.

Inside Room 13¶

Nakoa searched the room carefully.

At first, nothing appeared unusual. The room looked unoccupied but maintained, as though its resident expected to return without warning.

Near the bed, however, Nakoa’s attention was drawn to the rug. Beneath it, one of the floorboards had been disturbed. Fine green dust surrounded its edges, which the party believed might be another variety of veinstone dust.

Nakoa prised the board loose.

Hidden beneath it were several Veylrain wrappings and a brief handwritten note:

Hart / 13 / 3rd bell

The relic itself was gone.

Unwilling to end the search there, Nakoa moved to the dresser. Among the papers inside, he discovered a small personal information form.

It read:

Name: Derren Kerr
Birth Location: Karthrest
Occupation: Travelling metals broker

The writing on the form did not look natural. The name appeared to have been written over something else or altered after the form was originally completed.

Derren Kerr was almost certainly a false identity.

The Wrong Man¶

While Nakoa continued searching, Teclis and Cassian heard footsteps approaching along the corridor.

Someone was heading directly towards them.

This could be the occupant of room 13.

Thinking quickly, Teclis conjured the sound of a tremendous roar behind the approaching man. The illusion had the desired effect. Terrified, the man rushed forwards towards Teclis and Cassian, fumbling desperately with a set of keys.

The pair prepared to intercept him.

Instead, the frightened guest scrambled past them, unlocked room 10 and disappeared inside.

They had terrified an innocent hotel guest.

It was not their man.

Derren Kerr¶

With their search complete, Nakoa withdrew from room 13 and the party regrouped. They gathered the Veylrain wrappings, the note, the suspicious green dust and the forged personal details before heading downstairs.

They questioned Selda Morn about the room’s occupant.

Selda confirmed that room 13 was registered to Derren Kerr.

Kerr was not a recent guest. He had maintained the booking for some time and paid to keep room 13 available almost permanently, even during periods when he was apparently absent from the hotel.

The room was more than temporary accommodation.

It was a fixed meeting point, hiding place or drop location maintained under a false name.

The note beneath the floorboards suggested that another exchange was connected to the Gilded Hart at third bell, but whether it referred to an earlier meeting or one yet to take place remained unclear.

The Third Bell Debate¶

As the party prepared to leave for House Vaelisar under the cover of dark, Cassian stopped them.

What if the third bell did not refer to the day, but to the early hours of the morning?

What if Derren Kerr intended to return to room 13 at night?

If they left now, they might miss him entirely.

The group deliberated for a few tense moments. It was a reasonable fear and a dangerous possibility. In the end, however, they came to the conclusion that they had to risk it. Whatever waited inside House Vaelisar felt too urgent, too threatening and too important to ignore until morning.

They would move on the manor now.

Over the Wall¶

The party used what they had learned earlier about the manor’s exterior and its patrol patterns. Knowing that the interior patrols had thinned, they chose not to approach the front entrance and instead skirted the perimeter, moving through darkness towards the outer wall.

Nakoa went first.

With feline grace, he scaled the wall quickly and almost effortlessly. Just as he reached the top, he caught a paw on the stone and tumbled over the far side. Fortunately, instinct and agility took over. He flipped forward in one smooth motion and landed in a way that almost made the blunder look deliberate.

A shame none of the others saw it properly.

It had, in truth, looked rather swish.

Alekthar and Teclis followed with the aid of a rope.

Then came Cassian.

Cassian was not built for skulking. Heavy armour and stealth work had never been natural companions, and despite hauling himself over the wall with solid effort and strength, it was the descent that undid him. He came down hard on the other side with a heavy thud, landing on his feet but striking the ground with enough force that it seemed to shake beneath them.

The others froze.

Their eyes shot towards the front approach where the guards stirred at the sound. One of them turned his head and half-heartedly scanned the darkness, but the effort was lazy and uncertain. Whether through arrogance, carelessness or poor training, the outer watch failed to follow up properly.

The party had slipped in.

The Rear Approach¶

Quickly retrieving the rope, the group hurried after Alekthar through the gardens towards the rear of the manor, aiming for the gardening hut that connected to the larger house.

As they approached, they noticed something new.

A throbbing hum vibrated through the night air.

It was not loud, but it was impossible to ignore once heard. It was the sound of energy under pressure, a subtle and persistent thrum that seemed to press against the skin as much as the ear.

They found the rear doors unlocked.

Once inside, the strange hum became stronger. It did not come from directly ahead so much as from above and somewhere deeper within the structure, as though the house itself had become a resonating chamber for something unnatural.

They paused and looked upward, but could see nothing.

Then they pressed on.

Following the sound, they moved through the servant-side pathways and deeper into the manor’s interior.

The Central Hall¶

At last, they opened a door into the manor’s central hall.

The room beyond had been wrecked.

The central space was shattered and dilapidated, a noble chamber broken open into ruin. Wooden walls were splintered. Fine floor tiles had been cracked apart or torn loose entirely, the ground littered with fragments of ceramic and broken debris. Furniture had been tossed aside and smashed against the outer walls. Blood had been smeared across torn tapestries and painted surfaces, leaving dark streaks that seemed almost black in the moonlit gloom.

The hum was louder here than anywhere else.

The air was stale and unnaturally cold, especially for a summer’s night.

They looked upward.

The ceiling had been torn open, revealing a great hole into the upper floor where shattered boards hung jagged and broken. Beyond that, parts of the roof had given way as well, letting moonlight spill down into the wreckage.

And there, suspended in the ruined vertical space between the floors, hung the source of it all.

A black orb.

It floated motionless in the air, heavy and wrong, as if it had more weight than the room could bear and yet no need of support. Its surface was not smooth but fractured, the darkness split by dim veins of red that pulsed deep within like banked embers trapped beneath stone. Every so often a faint red crackle spilled across its shell, thin and momentary, gone almost as soon as it appeared.

The sound filling the hall came from it.

A low and ceaseless vibration. A presence rather than a noise.

The orb did not blaze.

It did not lash out.

It simply hung there in dreadful stillness, humming softly in the cold dark, as though it were waiting for the house, the night, or the people staring at it to realise what had already happened.

And with that sight before them, the session came to an end.
