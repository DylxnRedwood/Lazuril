# Caer Oryn & Morfael Gazetteer

*Player-facing gazetteer for Caer Oryn, capital of the Vermillion Crown, and Morfael, the river town pressed against its outer edge.*

Caer Oryn is the polished face of Vermillion power: a white-stone capital of bridges, permits, academies, soldiers, civic schools, toll offices, formal temples, river chains, and watched streets. It is wealthy, educated, organised, and proud of being all three. Its citizens are taught that prosperity comes from discipline, and that discipline requires schooling, service, law, and loyalty to the state.

Morfael is what sits below that order. It is close enough to the capital to be controlled by it, but far enough downriver and downhill to be ignored when convenient. It exists because Caer Oryn needs river labour, ferry crossings, customs records, cheap storage, fish markets, pauper burials, repair yards, and people willing to do work the upper city prefers not to see.

This gazetteer is written for players. It contains public knowledge, common assumptions, visible institutions, rumours, places people can visit, and details that characters might reasonably learn before or during early play. It does not include hidden histories, DM-only explanations, encounter tables, stat blocks, or secret faction structures.

## The Vermillion Crown

The Vermillion Crown is one of Arkhaven’s greatest powers. It is wealthy, highly organised, militarised, and human-led. Its strength comes from control of movement through the heart of the continent: roads, rivers, bridges, locks, ports, tolls, permits, fortified crossings, and regulated Godscar routes.

The Crown is currently at war with the Leridian Concord. To Vermillion citizens, the war is commonly described as a defence of order, passage rights, and continental stability. To its enemies, the Crown is a state that has turned geography into a weapon and movement into obedience.

The state is ruled by **Lord Protector Maeron Cadris**, whose full formal title is **Lord Protector Maeron Cadris, Guardian of the Godscar, Warden of the Crimson Chain, Supreme Marshal of the Crown Armies, Keeper of Caer Oryn**. Cadris took power twenty years ago through a coup. Before his rule, authority was divided between noble houses, military officers, trade officials, and regional governors. His rule ended that divided balance and replaced it with a centralised state built around discipline, education, military readiness, surveillance, and public loyalty.

Many citizens believe Cadris saved the Crown from noble paralysis and made it strong enough to survive its enemies. Others keep quieter views. In the Vermillion Crown, silence is often treated as good judgement.

## The Civic Directorate

The Civic Directorate is the central ruling system created after Maeron Cadris took power. It controls law, education, military administration, movement, taxation, infrastructure, state security, and the regulation of Godscar passage. To outsiders it can feel like a maze of seals and offices. To loyal citizens it is the machine that keeps roads open, schools funded, bridges guarded, locks working, and enemies contained.

The **Passage Directorate** controls roads, rivers, bridges, locks, tolls, travel permits, licensed ports, Crimson Chain movement, and Godscar crossings.

The **Treasury Directorate** controls taxation, state coinage, merchant charters, Crown contracts, banking oversight, and toll revenue.

The **Directorate of Instruction** controls public schooling, academies, civic education, officer training, approved histories, legal education, and scholarship grants.

The **Armature Directorate** controls military supply, armouries, officer postings, fortress planning, strategic weapons, and state weapons production.

The **Surveyor Directorate** controls maps, land records, censuses, water routes, mineral claims, Godscar studies, and infrastructure planning.

Most citizens do not interact with every branch, but almost everyone lives under the weight of at least one of them.

## Caer Oryn: Capital of the Crown

Caer Oryn is a vast hexagonal city of white stone built around the River Oryn. The river passes through the capital from east to west, beneath six monumental bridges, before continuing toward Lake Tharne and the western waters of the Godscar.

The city’s hexagonal design is deliberate: six sides, six major approaches, six fortified civic divisions, and six great bridges over the River Oryn. Even visitors who know nothing of Vermillion politics can see that Caer Oryn was built to express control.

The city is walled, but not simply sealed shut. Its strongest walls protect the main approaches, higher districts, military roads, and administrative centres. The riverfront is more open, because commerce demands movement, but it is heavily watched through quays, chain gates, patrol posts, customs houses, permit offices, and Crown officials who monitor what enters and leaves by water.

The lower districts contain docks, warehouses, markets, river labour offices, workshops, taverns, modest housing, public schools, and most of the working life of the city. These areas are busy, useful, and tightly managed rather than abandoned. Farther from the water, the city rises into wealthier and more controlled districts. At the centre stands the Crownward: the seat of command, law, archives, vaults, and national administration.

Caer Oryn has no true peasant quarter within its walls. Poverty exists, but it is managed through public labour, ration systems, civic schools, temple support, employment registers, and strict urban administration. This does not make the city kind. It makes the city efficient.

## Districts and Civic Divisions of Caer Oryn

### The Outer Wards

Residential districts, workshops, public schools, warehouses, river labour offices, markets, and modest but stable housing. The Outer Wards are where most ordinary citizens encounter the Crown’s promise: work, school, patrols, paperwork, and the expectation that useful people should remain useful.

### The Guild Quarter

Merchant houses, banking halls, guild courts, toll registries, caravan offices, trade brokers, and commercial offices. Wealth moves through the Guild Quarter in ledgers before it moves in coin. A damaged seal, unclear charter, or unpaid fee can trap a merchant here longer than a broken axle.

### The Academy Quarter

Schools, colleges, libraries, officer academies, legal halls, map archives, and magical institutes. Caer Oryn invests heavily in education because educated citizens become effective clerks, officers, engineers, merchants, surveyors, lawyers, administrators, and mages. Credentials matter here almost as much as blood.

### The Foundry Quarter

Armouries, inspection yards, military workshops, controlled foundries, river-chain mechanisms, and state weapon houses. The Foundry Quarter does not exist only to make blades. It supports the machinery of a passage state: chains, locks, hoists, bridge fittings, signal devices, and military equipment.

### The High Quarter

Ministerial estates, noble homes, embassies, treasury halls, high courts, theatres, civic temples, and formal gardens. The High Quarter is wealthy, polished, and careful. Even celebration here looks inspected.

### The Crownward

The central seat of power. It contains the Lord Protector’s palace and keep, the highest courts, national archives, command chambers, state vaults, and the main offices of the Civic Directorate. Citizens may speak of the Crownward with pride, but most do not expect to enter it without cause.

## Cadris' Day

**Cadris’ Day** is a public holiday held in Caer Oryn to celebrate Lord Protector Maeron Cadris’ coup and the beginning of the modern Vermillion Crown.

Officially, it is remembered as the day divided noble rule ended and the Crown was restored to strength, discipline, and purpose. Streets are dressed in Vermillion banners, civic schools hold public recitations, soldiers parade through the higher districts, and officials give speeches praising order, service, and loyalty to the state.

In the wealthier districts, Cadris’ Day is polished and formal. There are military displays, academy processions, public feasts, temple blessings, and carefully arranged celebrations beneath the eyes of the Directorate.

In the lower districts, the holiday is more mixed. Some people celebrate it honestly, especially those whose families prospered under Cadris’ rule. Others treat it as a day to keep quiet, hang the right colours, say the right words, and avoid being noticed.

The city calls it a celebration. Plenty of people understand it is also a reminder.

## Other Major Cities of the Vermillion Crown

Caer Oryn is the capital, but it is not the only city that matters.

**Karthrest** is a fortified lock-city where engineered water stairs force river traffic to slow, register, and pay before continuing through Crown-controlled passage routes. It is a counting machine with walls, bells, floodgates, clerks, chain crews, auditors, and soldiers.

**Ossengard** is a fortress city and officer-school centre on the hard road between the Crown’s interior estates and scar-facing military corridors. It is known for harsh military schooling, logistics training, siege discipline, map reading, winter marching, and the art of holding a road after retreat would be easier.

**Rhalstoke** is an industrial riverworks city that builds the chains, gears, bridge parts, pump housings, gate mechanisms, lock teeth, flood braces, pulleys, and iron fittings used across Crown-controlled routes. It smells of hot metal, wet timber, oil, coal dust, and river mud.

Together, these cities show what the Vermillion Crown values: movement, discipline, machinery, education, military readiness, and control.

## Morfael: The Wet Edge of the Capital

Morfael is a river town attached to the outer edge of Caer Oryn. It is close enough to feel like part of the capital, but far enough outside the main walls to be treated differently.

The town sits downstream of the city, where the River Oryn runs west toward Lake Tharne. Morfael grew where the capital did not want to look too closely. Smaller channels, drainage cuts, old sewer mouths, service roads, spillways, poorer river land, and half-official storage all collect here before the water reaches the lake.

Morfael is not lawless, but it is not Caer Oryn either. It has watch posts, toll points, river chains, customs sheds, patrol routes, and Crown officers. It does not have the same clean order as the higher districts inside the capital.

People come to Morfael when they need work, passage, cheap lodging, quiet trade, river access, or a place where the Crown is watching but not always listening. The town is crowded with boatmen, washerfolk, dock labourers, fishmongers, cart drivers, sewer workers, petty merchants, smugglers, shrine-keepers, beggars, guards, pilgrims, and people who cannot afford to live properly inside Caer Oryn.

Morfael should feel wet, lived-in, poor in places, useful to everyone, and trusted by no one. It is close to power, but rarely protected by it.

## Sites of Morfael

### The Flood Steps

Broad stone steps where the river meets the town. Fisherfolk, laundresses, children, dockhands, ferrymen, paupers, and market runners use these steps every day. During flood season, the lower steps vanish under brown-green water. Locals treat the Flood Steps as ordinary, which is exactly why anything strange there becomes everyone’s business.

### The Black Eel Market

A cramped fish and salvage market near the Flood Steps. Its stalls sell river fish, smoked eel, cheap knives, netting, hooks, repaired boots, recovered river goods, broken tools, and things no one asks too many questions about. It is not advertised as criminal, but everyone knows some goods arrive by routes that do not trouble official ledgers.

### Chapel of the Last Lantern

A small, practical chapel connected to **The Final Journey**. It serves drowned sailors, paupers, dock accidents, unidentified dead, grieving families, and those too poor to pay for formal rites in Caer Oryn. Its bell is old and cracked. Locals say it sounds different when rung for someone whose passing is not yet settled.

### Moon-in-Water Shrine

A small Selûnite alcove built into an old flood wall. A shallow basin holds river water. Sailors, ferrymen, widows, messengers, and travellers leave candles here before night crossings or long roads. It matters because ordinary people ask for safe passage here, write names of the missing, leave letters for those who may never return, and sit with uncertainty.

### The Red Lockhouse

A Crown-controlled customs and lock office near the canal mouth. Publicly, it handles tolls, cargo seals, river permits, lock schedules, late fees, and arguments about missing stamps. People in Morfael know the Red Lockhouse receives sealed orders that do not pass through the town reeve’s hands.

### The Kestrel Drain

A wide storm outflow barred with rusted iron. At low water, parts of it look large enough for a small creature or desperate person to crawl inside. Children are warned away from it. Adults pretend the warnings are about drowning, rats, and sickness.

### Harrowchain Ferry

A chain ferry linking Morfael to the opposite bank and several river paths used by labourers, smugglers, messengers, and people who prefer not to pass through Crown gates. It is slow, noisy, useful, and watched less carefully than officials claim.

### The Hook and Bell

A low tavern near the quay. It serves fish stew, sour beer, dockside gossip, and cheap beds. It is not safe, but it is useful. In Morfael, that is often enough.

### Vellum House

A narrow records office where cargo manifests, letters, false invoices, permits, dockside contracts, and discreet copies are written for people who cannot read or cannot safely use official channels. The line between legal help and forgery is thinner here than anyone admits.

### Cairnwell Yard

A repair yard for carts, barges, cracked wheels, pulley frames, chain posts, hooks, dock hoists, and practical metalwork. If something breaks in Morfael and cannot be carried to Caer Oryn, it usually passes through Cairnwell Yard.

### Low Lantern Row

A narrow row of lodging houses, cheap rooms, widows’ rooms, rented storage, and places where people arrive under one name and leave under another. People disappear here without always leaving town.

### Marrowgate Road

The road climbing from Morfael toward the controlled outer approaches of Caer Oryn. City watch, Crown agents, tax carts, legal messengers, military clerks, and official processions use this road more than locals do.

## Public Faces of Morfael

**Cerys Varrow** is the Crown-appointed reeve of Morfael. She manages taxes, dock complaints, street repairs, official notices, and the difficult work of answering to Caer Oryn while living among people who resent her authority.

**Watch-Corporal Dannel Strake** is a lower-town watch corporal. He is tired, cautious, and not as corrupt as people assume. He wants order first and truth second, which makes him frustrating but not useless.

**Sister Halva Dorn** is a dwarf lay-priest of the Chapel of the Last Lantern. She tends the poor dead, drowned sailors, unnamed bodies, and families who cannot afford the upper city. She is stern, compassionate, and difficult to intimidate.

**Eira Lorne** keeps the Moon-in-Water Shrine. She is not a grand priestess; she cleans the basin, lights candles, records names, comforts families, and remembers which riverfolk have not come home.

**Old Pell** is the ferryman of Harrowchain Ferry. He knows the river better than the town watch knows its streets, speaks slowly, and notices everything.

**Mara Venn** is a fishmonger at the Black Eel Market. She is loud, practical, and protective of local children. She knows who belongs in Morfael and who walks like they are pretending to belong.

**Rennic Sallow** is a halfling broker-scribe at Vellum House. He writes cargo manifests, letters, invoices, and dockside contracts for people who cannot or should not use official channels. He is useful, cautious, and rarely as harmless as he looks.

**Joric Marn** is a customs factor at the Red Lockhouse. He keeps records, stamps permits, and has mastered the official art of not understanding more than he is paid to understand.

**Elian Marr** is a retired lock engineer. He knows old waterworks, service routes, and outdated mechanisms better than many current officials.

**Maelis Wren** is an apothecary of Low Lantern Row. She sells poultices, sleep draughts, fever tinctures, dockhand liniment, and the kind of quiet help poor people seek before risking official attention.

## Law, Order, and Daily Control

Caer Oryn’s law is written, recorded, witnessed, and procedural. A document can matter more than a sword if the seal is correct. A missing seal can make honest cargo look criminal. A bad ledger entry can stall a family business for months.

Morfael’s law is rougher. The watch knows many offenders by name, but the town also knows when the watch chooses not to see something. Serious matters involving Crown officers, customs records, foreign travellers, military cargo, or suspicious river traffic can be pulled into Caer Oryn’s systems quickly. Once that happens, Morfael rarely hears the full story again.

The Passage Directorate is the branch ordinary travellers most often fear. It controls permits, crossings, lock schedules, tolls, licensed river movement, and the practical right to pass from one place to another without being stopped.

The public lesson is simple: movement through the Vermillion Crown is possible, profitable, and protected, but it is never truly free.

## Economy and Trade

Caer Oryn turns movement into power. Its wealth comes from river tolls, merchant charters, legal authority, civic schooling, military administration, academy training, public contracts, bridge control, lock control, customs enforcement, and the ability to make everyone else’s passage depend on Crown systems.

Morfael survives on the physical work behind that wealth. Fish are smoked. Ropes are tarred. Ferries are poled. Carts are repaired. Bodies are washed. Letters are written. Cargo is shifted. Drains are cleared. Barges are patched. Names are remembered.

The capital counts value in ledgers. Morfael counts it in favours, names, routes, and who will still open a door after dark.

## Religion and Magic

The Vermillion Crown worships many gods, especially those associated with law, victory, wealth, civilisation, knowledge, craft, trade, protection, and war. Religious institutions are permitted and respected, but they operate under state law.

Kelemvor’s presence is especially visible through The Final Journey and its concern for proper death, lawful passage, funerary dignity, and the care of those who cannot afford grand rites. In Morfael, this work is practical rather than ceremonial. The dead still need washing whether or not anyone important knew their names.

Selûne’s worship is quieter but deeply felt along the river. Travellers, sailors, night workers, widows, messengers, and people waiting for someone overdue are drawn to her lamps and water-shrines.

Magic is common in the Crown, but regulated. It is taught, licensed, recorded, and directed toward useful service: medicine, architecture, investigation, mining, secure communication, military planning, education, and infrastructure. Unlicensed or unexplained magic draws attention quickly.

Veinstone remains part of the Crown’s public history. Earlier Vermillion rulers used metals infused with veinstone from cliffs and riverbeds around the Godscar to create magical weaponry that helped establish the state’s military reputation. In the modern era, citizens are told the Crown’s strength rests less on any single material and more on control, wealth, schooling, military organisation, and command of movement.

## Culture and Status

Vermillion culture is wealthy, educated, ambitious, and orderly. Status is shown through education, military rank, property, licensed trade rights, academy credentials, public service, and proximity to the Civic Directorate.

The wealthy display success through white-stone houses, private tutors, riverfront properties, fine clothing, formal patronage, and access to restricted markets. Modest families work hard to place children in civic schools, trade academies, officer programmes, or state apprenticeships.

The Crown does not present itself as cruel. Many citizens live comfortable lives and genuinely believe Maeron Cadris brought stability, safety, and national greatness. The danger of the Vermillion Crown lies in how much comfort depends on surveillance, controlled movement, military strength, and obedience.

Morfael’s culture is less polished and more direct. People there value usefulness, memory, and practical loyalty. A person who can mend a chain, pole a ferry, calm a grieving widow, read a current, forge a pass, wash a body, or keep a secret has value. A person who only carries fine papers may still be left standing in the rain.

## Common Talk and Local Rumours

Some Morfael families keep private lists of missing riverfolk because they do not trust the watch to remember the poor.

A covered barge is said to have passed the quay before dawn with no lanterns and men aboard wearing plain cloaks over military boots.

People have seen red mineral grit near the Flood Steps and the Kestrel Drain after rain. Officials call it runoff, brick dust, or cargo residue depending on who asks.

A child near the water claimed he saw gold light under a half-drowned man’s skin. Adults have repeated the story often enough to mock it, but not enough to forget it.

The Red Lockhouse receives sealed orders that even the town reeve is not permitted to read.

A Crown buyer recently purchased unusual medical supplies in Morfael, including clotting agents, sedatives, surgical spirits, and jars of red mineral residue.

Old people remember stranger things being carried into the Red Lockhouse months before the current rumours began.

Rivermen say the Harrowchain Ferry knows more secrets than any confessor in Caer Oryn.

No one in Morfael believes every rumour. No one sensible ignores all of them either.

## Using Caer Oryn and Morfael in Play

Caer Oryn should feel formal, prosperous, educated, militarised, and watched. It is not a ruined city or a nest of open chaos. Its threat comes from order, procedure, competence, pressure, and the fact that many citizens benefit from the system enough to defend it.

Morfael should feel wet, crowded, useful, suspicious, and human. It is not merely a slum. It is a working town that keeps the capital moving while absorbing the things Caer Oryn does not want on display.

Together, they create a strong campaign gateway. Caer Oryn provides power, law, academies, ministries, records, temples, military command, and state ambition. Morfael provides witnesses, river trouble, missing people, hidden cargo, poor chapels, salvage markets, unofficial routes, and the first signs that something beneath the Crown’s order is beginning to surface.
