# Arch-Lector Meren Voss

_Private NPC working page for Arch-Lector Meren Voss, Keeper of the Last Gate. This file supports the development of [Teclis Nimule Sílorion](../PCs/Teclis%20Nimule%20Sílorion.md), the politics of The Final Journey, and investigations involving death, false passage and possible undeath in [Caer Oryn](../Caer%20Oryn.md)._

Arch-Lector Meren Voss is the presiding authority of **The Final Journey**, a Kelemvorite church in Caer Oryn, capital of [The Vermillion Crown](../The%20Vermillion%20Crown.md). His formal title is **Keeper of the Last Gate**, reflecting his role as guardian of rites concerning death, judgement, burial order, the passage of souls and the church's institutional discipline.

Voss is not designed as a villain. He is an obstacle, a superior, a doctrinal counterweight and a human religious authority forced to share space with an elven illusionist he neither requested nor fully trusts.

## Appearance

Meren Voss is a lean human man approaching sixty, although prolonged exposure to death, grief and divine magic makes his true age difficult to judge. He is tall without being physically imposing and carries himself with the rigid posture of someone accustomed to standing through funerals that last for hours.

His face is narrow and severe, with deep lines running from the corners of his nose to his mouth. His skin has a muted, almost ashen quality, not from illness, but from decades spent inside crypts, mortuaries and candlelit sanctuaries. His hair is iron-grey, cut close to the scalp, and his beard is kept equally short. Nothing about him is permitted to become disordered.

His eyes are dark grey and unusually steady. He does not stare through people. He looks directly at them, patiently and without embarrassment, as though waiting for everything unnecessary to fall out of their explanation.

Voss wears layered charcoal robes beneath a heavy black mantle. The edges are embroidered in bone-white thread with closed gates, balanced scales and the names of former Keepers of the Last Gate. His holy symbol is a plain silver disc depicting a skeletal hand holding balanced scales. It is polished but unadorned.

He carries a long crozier of dark wood capped with a small black iron gate. It is not primarily a weapon, although anyone familiar with powerful clergy would recognise that he does not need to draw steel to be dangerous.

His power is most apparent through absence. Flames become still around him. Echoes seem shorter. A distressed person's breathing unconsciously slows when he places a hand upon them. When he invokes Kelemvor's authority, the room does not darken dramatically. Instead, everything becomes quiet enough to hear one's own heartbeat.

Voss is a high-ranking Grave Domain cleric who has performed final rites for nobles, soldiers, murderers, plague victims and unidentified bodies. He has questioned spirits, contained the risen dead and stood beside people during their final moments without flinching. He is capable of calling upon extraordinary divine power, but considers such power a responsibility rather than a display.

He does not look like a necromancer.

He looks like the man sent to make certain the necromancer's work is ended properly.

## Public Role

To the public, Meren Voss is solemn, controlled and difficult to impress. He presents himself as a man who has spent too long with grief to be moved by theatrics. His authority comes from ritual consistency, legal recognition and the trust of those who bring their dead to The Final Journey when all other arguments have ended.

Within the church, he governs rites, clergy discipline, doctrinal instruction, funerary judgement, testimony connected to death and the handling of religious disputes that touch upon souls, undeath, false passage, improper burial or claims of divine judgement.

His voice carries weight because he rarely raises it. He does not waste public anger. When he rebukes someone, it should feel like a door closing.

## General Demeanour

Voss is restrained, severe and almost painfully patient.

He rarely interrupts. He allows people to speak until they reveal the weakness in their own account. His silence is not passive. It is pressure.

He does not raise his voice when angry. His sentences become shorter.

He particularly dislikes:

- Exaggeration.
- Euphemisms used to avoid responsibility.
- Theatricality during serious matters.
- People describing their own actions collectively when personal accountability is required.
- Magical cleverness being presented as moral justification.
- Claims of necessity made after easier procedures were ignored.
- People who treat the sacred as a puzzle designed to prove their own intelligence.

When Teclis says, "We questioned him," Voss asks:

> "What did you do?"

When Teclis says, "The prisoner died," Voss corrects him:

> "You killed him. Continue."

He does not insult Teclis' intelligence. In some respects, that makes his criticism worse. Voss accepts that Teclis understood what he was doing and judges him accordingly.

## Core Belief

Meren Voss believes death must be met plainly.

For him, the dead deserve order, not performance. The grieving deserve solemnity, not clever demonstrations. The church deserves reverence, not constant institutional testing by outsiders who believe scepticism is the same thing as wisdom.

This does not mean Voss is naive. He understands false miracles, forged relics, manipulated testimony and religious corruption exist. He accepts, at least intellectually, why the [Scriptor Compact](../The%20Scriptor%20Compact.md) was created after the [War of the False Saints](../War%20of%20the%20False%20Saints.md). His issue is not with the existence of truth. His issue is with the method.

Voss fears that if the church uses deception too freely to expose deception, it will train the faithful to distrust every sacred thing. He believes Teclis underestimates the damage that can be done when a priesthood appears to prove that grief, visions, final words and holy signs might all be stagecraft.

## Relationship with Teclis

Meren Voss is the immediate institutional superior of [Teclis Nimule Sílorion](../PCs/Teclis%20Nimule%20Sílorion.md) within The Final Journey, but he is not Teclis' spiritual master.

This distinction is central.

Teclis' position exists because of old compact and creed connected to the Scriptor Compact. As a non-religious instrument of inquiry, illusion and doctrinal enforcement, Teclis is allowed to operate inside the church without personally bending the knee to Kelemvor.

Meren Voss tolerates this because he must. He does not respect it as a natural part of the church's spiritual life.

To Voss, death is sacred, judgement is solemn and the passage of souls should not require an elven illusionist standing in the margins, questioning testimony, reconstructing scenes, challenging doctrine and proving that mortal senses can be deceived. Voss sees Teclis as an intrusion into the church's spiritual authority, even when Teclis is technically within his rights.

Voss does not undermine Teclis at every opportunity, because that would make him petty. Instead, he sets boundaries, demands discipline, restricts access when religious dignity is at stake and reminds Teclis that legality is not the same thing as belonging.

## View of Illusion

Voss does not consider illusion inherently evil, but he finds it spiritually dangerous.

In private, he may concede that illusion can expose fraud. In public, he would never want the church to look dependent upon it. A Kelemvorite institution that leans too heavily on an elven illusionist risks appearing uncertain in its own doctrine.

His discomfort with Teclis is therefore not merely personal prejudice. It is institutional anxiety. Teclis represents a necessary tool that, if overused, could weaken the very authority it is meant to defend.

Voss is not simply annoyed by Teclis' arrogance. He is afraid Teclis might be right in ways that make faith harder to govern.

## Current Opinion after Garron Duskholt

Teclis' recent actions have strengthened Voss' belief that the Scriptor appointment is an unnecessary and potentially dangerous intrusion into Kelemvorite authority.

From Voss' perspective, Teclis was appointed to scrutinise testimony, identify magical deception and prevent religious authority from being abused. Instead, he used illusion to terrorise a restrained prisoner, participated in an interrogation outside recognised legal authority and then personally executed the prisoner.

The guillotine is particularly offensive to Voss. It used the image of death and final judgement as a theatrical instrument of coercion. It reduced something Voss considers sacred and inevitable into a stage prop.

The killing of Garron is more serious still.

Voss will not accept arguments that Garron was remorseless, dangerous or deserving. Those matters might justify arrest, trial or even lawful execution. They do not automatically give Teclis the right to pronounce and carry out a sentence.

His judgement of Teclis is likely to be:

> "You were appointed to expose the misuse of authority. You appear to have mistaken that appointment for authority of your own."

Voss now sees Teclis as evidence for his longstanding objection to the Compact. A Scriptor arrives claiming to protect institutions from unchecked belief, yet operates with little accountability himself.

However, Voss is not foolish enough to dismiss Teclis completely. The Obsidian Register, veinstone experimentation and the irregularities around House Vaelisar are credible threats. Teclis may possess exactly the skills needed to uncover them.

This frustrates Voss immensely.

He may continue allowing Teclis to investigate, but under stricter boundaries. His position becomes:

> "You may be necessary. Do not mistake necessity for approval."

## Strengths

Voss is institutionally powerful inside The Final Journey. He knows its clergy, records, obligations, donors, burial rights, doctrinal precedents and political vulnerabilities.

He is difficult to manipulate emotionally. He has seen too much grief to be easily swayed by spectacle. He listens for what people omit, not only what they say.

He has social authority in Caer Oryn because death touches every class. Nobles, officers, merchants, labourers, prisoners and soldiers all eventually pass through systems that men like Voss oversee.

He is also more intelligent than Teclis initially wants to admit.

## Weaknesses

Voss may mistake caution for wisdom.

His reverence for order can make him slow to accept that disorder has already entered the church. He may resist Teclis not because Teclis is wrong, but because Teclis' methods make the truth harder to contain once revealed.

He can also be politically vulnerable. The Final Journey operates within The Vermillion Crown, a state built on law, surveillance, controlled movement and civic authority. If a death, miracle claim, false passage or doctrinal dispute has Crown implications, Voss may be pressured from above while also trying to keep Teclis from overreaching.

His greatest weakness may be pride of another kind. Teclis is openly ambitious, but Voss may be just as proud in his certainty that solemnity makes him safer than brilliance.

# Teclis' Report after Session 3

## The Meeting

Voss should receive Teclis privately rather than rebuking him before the clergy.

The meeting takes place in Voss' audience chamber behind the main chapel. The room is austere, containing a long stone table, several sealed death registers, a basin of clean water and a single lamp.

Voss has already received a brief report from Morfael, most likely through Sister Halva Dorn, Watch-Corporal Dannel Strake or church correspondence. He knows that Edrin Vaal died, that Teclis was involved and that concerns regarding unnatural death have been raised.

He does not necessarily know the full account of Garron unless Teclis tells him.

Voss begins without offering refreshments or asking after Teclis' health.

> "You left this church as its appointed Scriptor. You have returned carrying rumours of unnatural death, a dead courier and enough unanswered questions to trouble two districts. Begin at Morfael. Omit nothing."

He lets Teclis give his initial account.

He then separates the story into individual actions, decisions and consequences.

## Why Teclis Did Not Report Earlier

Voss asks why Teclis continued moving between Morfael, Magpie Lane and the Academy Quarter without reporting the possibility of undeath immediately.

He understands that events moved quickly, but he considers potential undeath a direct matter for the church.

> "The possibility of undeath is not an incidental observation to be placed at the end of an adventurer's report. It is the reason this church maintains sealed crypts, grave wardens and rites of containment."

He wants to know whether Teclis considered informing The Final Journey and consciously chose not to, or simply never thought of it.

The second answer may concern him more.

## Questions concerning Edrin Vaal

Voss demands a precise account of Edrin's condition:

- Was Edrin breathing when first found?
- Was a pulse checked?
- How long was he believed dead?
- Was he cold?
- Did rigor or decomposition appear present?
- Did his wounds bleed normally when he moved?
- Did he speak coherently?
- Did he recognise anyone?
- Did he retain memories?
- Was his behaviour purposeful or compulsive?
- Did healing magic affect him normally?
- Did anyone use magic capable of identifying undead?
- What happened to his body after his final death?
- Was the red material present before or after he moved?

Voss repeatedly separates observation from interpretation.

> "Do not tell me he appeared dead. Tell me what his body did."

## The Guillotine Illusion

Voss does not object merely because it was an illusion.

He objects because Teclis used the symbolism of execution to force testimony from a restrained man.

> "You fashioned death into scenery."

> "The Compact permits deception in the examination of falsehood. It does not appoint you torturer."

> "You did not demonstrate that Garron's senses were unreliable. You made him believe his life would end unless he told you what you wanted."

If Teclis argues that the information proved accurate, Voss responds:

> "Accuracy does not cleanse the method that produced it."

## Garron's Execution

Voss asks Teclis to recount the killing personally and in exact terms.

He is disturbed by the selection of a number and the three magical blades. To him, this was not a hurried act of self-defence. It was ritualised and deliberately memorable.

> "You asked a bound man to participate in the performance of his own death."

> "That was not judgement. It was theatre wearing judgement's robes."

If Teclis says Garron deserved death:

> "Perhaps he did. That was not yours to decide alone."

If Teclis says Garron remained dangerous:

> "Then you deliver him to those equipped to hold dangerous men."

If Teclis says the Watch could not be trusted:

> "Then you bring him here."

If Teclis invokes the authority of the Scriptor Compact:

> "The Compact gives you standing. It does not give you absolution."

## The Obsidian Register

Voss takes the existence of the Obsidian Register seriously, particularly once Teclis describes the branded symbol, Edrin's condition and the use of veinstone.

He wants the following recorded:

- Garron Duskholt's full name.
- A drawing of the crying-eye brand.
- Odran Kesh's name.
- The location of the Counting House.
- The Gilded Hart and room 13.
- The route through the drains.
- The connection to Edrin Vaal.
- Any known Crown involvement.
- Every sample or fragment of veinstone currently held by the party.

Voss may already suspect that an organisation capable of altering living bodies will eventually cross into matters traditionally associated with undeath, whether intentionally or not.

## House Vaelisar

The report of body bags leaving House Vaelisar immediately changes Voss' focus.

The Final Journey is involved in death registration, funerary preparation, burial rights and the lawful transfer of remains. Numerous bodies being removed from a noble estate without corresponding records would be a major irregularity.

Voss asks:

- How many body bags were seen?
- Who saw them?
- Which entrance was used?
- Were they taken by the Watch, Crown officials, private servants or unknown carriers?
- Where were they taken?
- Were any deaths officially registered?
- Were funeral rites requested?
- Were the bodies identified?

This is one area where Voss can become a valuable ally. He can quietly examine church death rolls, mortuary intake records, burial permissions and requests for unnamed remains.

If no corresponding records exist, he says:

> "Bodies do not disappear merely because a noble house closes its gates. Someone has removed the dead from the lawful order of this city."

# Reaction to Possible Undeath

Voss does not dismiss the possibility of undeath.

He considers it completely plausible.

However, he refuses to use the word casually.

To Voss, undeath is not a general description for anything that survives an apparently fatal injury. It is a specific disruption of death involving the body, soul, animating force or an external necromantic will.

> "Undeath is not impossible, Scriptor. It is specific."

Based on the current evidence, Voss would probably suspect that Edrin was not technically undead. His provisional assessment would be one of the following:

- Edrin was never fully dead and entered a deathlike state.
- Veinstone forced his body to continue functioning beyond its natural limit.
- The substance produced an arcane or alchemical overdrive resembling reanimation.
- His body was being compelled while his soul remained present but impaired.
- Some external command or magical trigger overrode normal bodily failure.

Voss may describe it as:

> "A false passage."

Or:

> "A living body driven beyond the boundary at which it should have stopped."

Or:

> "Unquiet, perhaps. Undead, not yet proven."

This distinction matters enormously to him. True undead may require consecration, destruction of an animating force, protection of the soul and specialised containment. A veinstone victim may require healing, extraction, magical suppression or treatment as a living casualty.

Using the wrong classification could result in living victims being burned or destroyed as undead.

Voss is especially concerned about public panic.

> "If frightened citizens hear that the dead are walking, they will not wait for us to determine which bodies still contain living souls."

He orders that the matter remain confidential until examined.

## Voss' Likely Conclusion

Edrin Vaal was probably neither naturally alive nor truly undead at the moment of his violent resurgence. His body had been pushed into a condition that violated the expected boundary of death without necessarily severing or replacing the soul.

This concerns Voss at least as much as conventional necromancy.

> "This may not be undeath. That does not make it less profane."

# Actions Voss Would Take

Voss requests access to Edrin's remains, assuming they are recoverable.

He may personally examine the corpse or assign trusted grave clergy to do so. His investigation could include:

- Examination for necromantic residue.
- Determining whether the soul can be contacted.
- Establishing whether Edrin truly died before his violent resurgence.
- Testing the body's response to consecrated magic.
- Preserving the remains against actual reanimation.
- Comparing the corpse with other unexplained deaths.
- Examining the red material without allowing it to contaminate the church.
- Reviewing recent burial and mortuary records for similar cases.

His divine senses might tell him that no true undead presence inhabits the remains. That does not reassure him.

It means someone has discovered another way of violating the natural boundary of death.

> "If Vaal's soul had departed and something else moved his flesh, we will know."

> "If his soul remained and the stone drove him onward, then someone has built a torture device from the boundary between life and death."

> "Either answer concerns me."

# Restrictions Placed on Teclis

Voss does not suspend Teclis because the investigation is too important and the Compact may prevent unilateral removal.

Instead, he imposes conditions:

1. Teclis must submit a written account of Edrin Vaal, Garron Duskholt and Magpie Lane.
2. Teclis must not invoke the authority of The Final Journey while conducting unauthorised interrogations.
3. Any further evidence of unnatural death must be reported to Voss immediately.
4. Corpses, remains or suspected undead connected to the investigation must be surrendered to the church for examination.
5. Teclis is not to execute restrained prisoners.
6. A priest or grave warden may accompany Teclis during future investigations involving suspected undeath.
7. Teclis must provide the church with copies of all drawings, notes and evidence related to the Obsidian Register.

Voss frames this not as punishment, but as institutional containment.

> "Your work will continue. It will no longer continue unwitnessed."

# Responses to Teclis' Attitude

## If Teclis Is Honest

Voss remains angry, but develops a small amount of respect for Teclis accepting responsibility.

> "Confession does not erase consequence. It does, however, permit us to begin dealing with it."

He continues the investigation and may protect Teclis from immediate external scrutiny while deciding what must be reported.

## If Teclis Is Arrogant

Voss becomes colder and more formal.

> "Your intelligence has never been in question. Your judgement now is."

He may begin documenting Teclis' conduct for future communication with Lanthir Súranel or the authorities overseeing the Compact.

## If Teclis Is Remorseful

Voss does not comfort him.

> "Regret is useful only when it changes the next decision."

He may give Teclis an opportunity to assist with Edrin's examination or stand through the man's proper funerary rites.

## If Teclis Defends Everything as Necessary

> "Necessity is the word intelligent men use when they have grown impatient with restraint."

## If Teclis Accuses Voss of Ignoring the Threat

> "I am not ignoring it. I am refusing to become it."

# Useful Lines for Voss

> "Begin again. This time without the word 'we.'"

> "You were sent here to distinguish truth from spectacle. Instead, you used spectacle to manufacture truth."

> "A confession made beneath an imagined blade remains a confession made beneath a blade."

> "You do not honour truth by removing every person who might later contradict your version of it."

> "Garron Duskholt may have been a murderer. That did not make you his judge."

> "I have no objection to your questions. I object to the corpses you leave behind them."

> "The dead do not offend me, Teclis. The living are usually more troublesome."

> "Do not bring me rumours of walking corpses. Bring me temperatures, wounds, witnesses and remains."

> "Undeath is not a metaphor for something you failed to understand."

> "Your office may be unnecessary. Unfortunately, the people you are investigating appear determined to prove otherwise."

> "You may continue because this city may need what you are. Do not mistake that for approval."

# Best Closing Beat

After the questioning, Voss turns to the sealed death registers concerning House Vaelisar.

He places one hand upon the top volume and says:

> "No deaths have been declared from that house."

He looks back towards Teclis.

> "You say body bags were removed."

After Teclis confirms it, Voss opens the register.

> "Then, for the moment, Scriptor, we have found something upon which we agree."

He instructs Teclis to return the following morning with his full notes, the veinstone evidence and anyone who personally witnessed Edrin's condition.

Before dismissing him, Voss adds:

> "Whatever happened inside House Vaelisar, someone has attempted to place its dead beyond record, rite and judgement."

> "That is now a matter for The Final Journey."

# Use in Play

Meren Voss should create friction without blocking the story.

He can deny Teclis easy access to a corpse until rites are complete. He can insist that an illusion demonstration occur behind closed doors rather than before mourners. He can order Teclis to observe rather than intervene during a sacred proceeding. He can require written justification before allowing church records to be removed, copied or tested magically.

He can also be useful. When convinced, Voss can provide authority, protection, access to church records and a sober human reading of events Teclis might reduce too quickly to technique.

The best Voss scenes force Teclis to decide whether being correct is enough, or whether truth must sometimes be revealed in a way that people can survive.

# Possible Secrets and Future Development

- Voss may have previously seen a false miracle destroy a family, temple or investigation, explaining his distrust of spectacle.
- Voss may have once defended the Scriptor Compact publicly, only to regret how much authority it gave outsiders inside human faiths.
- Voss may know more about Teclis' assignment than he admits, including which elven patrons pushed for this particular posting.
- Voss may have enemies inside The Vermillion Crown who would welcome a public dispute between him and Teclis.
- Voss may eventually become one of Teclis' most important allies, but only if Teclis learns that reverence is not always ignorance and Voss learns that scrutiny is not always contempt.
