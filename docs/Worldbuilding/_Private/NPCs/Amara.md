## Amara

_Private NPC working page for Amara. This page supports [Nakoa](Nakoa.md), family continuity, relic appraisal, Veylrath history, and the domestic consequences of dangerous curiosity._

Amara is [Nakoa](Nakoa.md)'s mother, once a relic appraiser and historian before settling more fully into family life in Tethvale. She is warm, sharp-eyed, and almost impossible to fool. Of all Nakoa's family, she may be the one most able to recognise that his curiosity is not simply mischief, and that the relic incident has changed more than his behaviour.

Amara encouraged each of her children to become what they truly were rather than what family or clan expectation might find convenient. That kindness is not weakness. She believes in self-knowledge, not indulgence. If a child becomes themselves by ignoring every warning, she is capable of naming that honestly.

## Past Work

Before family life became her centre, Amara worked as a relic appraiser and historian. She learned how to identify artifacts, read material clues, recognise false provenance, respect handling procedures, and distinguish between objects that are merely old and objects that remain active in some dangerous way.

This background matters for Nakoa. His fascination with strange objects and old places did not appear from nowhere. Amara likely nurtured the first healthy version of that interest. She may have shown him how to look closely, how to ask where an object came from, and how to understand that history survives through fragments.

She would also have taught him that touching an unknown relic is foolish.

## Family

Amara is partnered with [Pathspeaker Tarek](Pathspeaker%20Tarek.md), whose routecraft and river knowledge complement her historical and appraising background. Together they raised [Kaelo](Kaelo.md), [Nakoa](Nakoa.md), [Suri](Suri.md), [Riven](Riven.md), and [Enzo](Enzo.md).

She likely sees each child clearly. Kaelo carries duty. Nakoa carries dangerous curiosity. Suri and Riven carry focused competence. Enzo carries performance, warmth, and a storyteller's appetite for impossible embellishment.

Amara's love is personal, but her judgement is precise. She does not need official reports to know when one of her children is hiding something.

## Relationship to Nakoa

Amara may be one of Nakoa's strongest emotional anchors. She encouraged his nature more than many adults would have, which means her disappointment would cut deeply if she discovered he used that encouragement as permission to ignore all discipline.

She is unlikely to condemn him without listening. She is also unlikely to accept a charming half-truth. Nakoa may be able to fool guards, strangers, and even friends, but Amara should be difficult to deceive in any scene where she has time to look at him properly.

If she sees the golden flecks in his markings, she may understand faster than others that this is not ordinary arcane talent.

## Personality

Amara is warm, perceptive, and quietly formidable. Her gentleness does not soften her intelligence. She has a way of asking questions that makes excuses collapse under their own weight.

She values truth, but she also understands fear. This makes her more dangerous than a scolding authority figure. She may be compassionate enough that Nakoa wants to confess and perceptive enough that he knows lying would be pointless.

## Use in Play

Amara can be used as family pressure, emotional grounding, relic-context support, and a route into older Veylrath scholarship. She may also know [Seraya Venn](Seraya%20Venn.md) through relic circles, appraisal discussions, or preservation work.

She is useful when the story needs someone who loves Nakoa but refuses to romanticise what he has done. She can help him, but her help may come with conditions: tell the full truth, bring the matter before the right people, stop treating the relic as a private adventure, and accept that old objects can leave debts behind.

## Secrets and Future Development

Amara may recognise something in Nakoa's altered markings that others miss.

She may have appraised an object with similar golden etching years before.

She may know family marking traditions that suggest Nakoa's bloodline was relevant to the relic.

She may have left relic work because of an incident involving an object that should not have been handled.
