# Hall of Twelve Dawns

> [!warning] Private DM Notes
> This page contains the truth of the Vaelisar massacre, the failed binding beneath [[House Vaelisar]], the Sharite Eclipse Rite, the black orb, the death of all twelve Dawnmantles, and the relationship between the Vaelisar Dawnmantle and the Bound Radiance of [[Alekthar of House Amakiir|Alekthar's]] family.
>
> The players should not be told immediately that all twelve Dawnmantles are dead. The hidden tome was written before the massacre and describes them as living. The physical chamber gradually contradicts the text.

## Summary

**Location:** Directly beneath the billiards room of [[House Vaelisar]]  
**Entrance:** Beneath the large green carpet, through a concealed stone hatch and down the staircase  
**Formal name:** The Hall of Twelve Dawns  
**Family name:** The Lower Hall  
**Ritual:** The Rite of the Carried Dawn  
**Hidden tome:** The Concordance of Twelve Dawns  
**Required Dawnmantles:** Twelve living, fully inducted bearers  
**Actual current Dawnmantles:** None  
**Cause of failure:** All twelve living bearers were ritually killed during a Sharite inversion of the Dawnmantle rite  
**Result:** The Vaelisar seal-thread was destroyed and its released celestial power was redirected into the black orb above

The Hall of Twelve Dawns is the ritual heart of House Vaelisar. It exists to maintain twelve simultaneous living connections between the Vaelisar bloodline and the ancient lattice that helps imprison the old god beneath Lazuril.

The Vaelisar family believed the Hall was an ancestral obligation tied to their celestial blood and noble inheritance. Only a small number understood that it formed one section of a much greater binding maintained by several aasimar houses.

The Sharites understood enough to weaponise it.

They gathered the complete circle of twelve Dawnmantles, inverted the rite, killed every bearer and used the simultaneous collapse of the twelve living bonds to form the black orb upstairs.

The orb is a planar wound. It connects to another plane and releases aberrations corrupted by the influence of the imprisoned old god.

# The Hidden Truth

## The Purpose of the Aasimar Houses

The old aasimar houses do not merely preserve ceremonial celestial traditions.

Each family maintains part of an ancient living seal around the old god imprisoned beneath Lazuril. Their inherited rites bind celestial blood into a wider lattice of bloodlines, locations, vows and ritual stations.

No single family maintains the entire prison.

Each house holds one portion of it.

Examples include:

- House Vaelisar and the twelvefold Dawnmantle.
- House Amakiir and the Bound Radiance.
- Cael Tharos and its own inherited seal-work.
- Other houses shown upon the Dawnline Map, some active, some weakened and some already destroyed.

If one family fails, the prison weakens.

If all of the binding houses fail, the old god is released.

## What Happened at House Vaelisar

Immediately before the manor incident:

- All twelve Dawnmantle stations were occupied.
- The scattered bearers had been recalled to House Vaelisar.
- Tarian Vaelisar had recently been inducted to replace Rathan Vaelisar.
- Other relatives, spouses, attendants and household witnesses were also present.
- The family believed it was conducting an emergency strengthening of the Dawnmantle.
- Sharites had infiltrated or compromised the preparations.
- The legitimate rite was inverted into an Eclipse Rite.
- All twelve fully inducted Dawnmantles were killed in a coordinated ritual.
- Other family members were killed to prevent quick restoration of the line and remove witnesses.
- The released celestial power was redirected upward through the manor foundations.
- The black orb formed above the Hall.

The complete Vaelisar seal-thread has failed.

The wider prison survives only because other aasimar houses still maintain their parts of the lattice.

## What Happened at Cael Tharos

The attack upon Alekthar's home used the same general plan.

The perpetrators successfully killed all fully inducted holders of the Bound Radiance. They attempted to convert the collapse of that family binding into another black orb.

The ritual failed because Alekthar's sister was included in the sacrificial arrangement despite not being fully inducted.

Her connection had begun, but it had not been completed.

The attackers therefore did not kill an exact and complete ritual set. One place in the pattern contained a partially connected participant rather than a true Bound Radiance holder.

The result was:

- The fully inducted Bound Radiance holders died.
- The Amakiir seal-thread was devastated.
- The released power did not form a stable planar wound.
- No black orb appeared.
- An unresolved fragment of the binding remained through Alekthar's sister's incomplete induction.
- The perpetrators did not initially understand why the ritual failed.

At House Vaelisar, they corrected their mistake.

They waited until the Vaelisar ring contained exactly twelve fully inducted living holders. They then killed the complete set.

That is why the Vaelisar ritual succeeded.

# Entrance from the Billiards Room

The Hall is reached from the billiards room of House Vaelisar.

The large green carpet conceals a broad stone hatch set directly into the floor. The carpet was cut and fitted specifically to hide the rectangular seam around it.

The billiards table stands slightly off-centre so that it does not rest directly over the hatch.

## Finding the Entrance

Characters searching the billiards room may notice:

- The green carpet does not lie completely flat near its centre.
- One edge has been lifted and reset more recently than the others.
- The billiards table is oddly positioned within the room.
- The floor sounds hollow beneath the carpet.
- Pale stone dust clings to the underside of the carpet.
- Faint wear marks show that the billiards table has repeatedly been moved.

### Checks

- **Perception DC 12:** Notices that the table is positioned awkwardly and the carpet rises slightly at its centre.
- **Investigation DC 13:** Determines that something broad is concealed beneath the carpet.
- **Investigation DC 14:** Finds the carpet pins and the edge of the hatch without damaging the carpet.
- **Strength DC 12:** Moves the billiards table far enough to expose the hatch.
- **Two or more characters working together:** Move the table without a check.
- **Knocking on the floor:** Immediately reveals the hollow space beneath the carpet.

## The Stone Hatch

Rolling back the carpet reveals a rectangular hatch of dark grey stone bordered with narrow brass strips.

Its upper surface is carved with twelve worn sunbursts arranged around a central black circle.

A recessed iron lifting ring sits inside the circle.

The hatch is heavy but counterweighted from below.

It was built for concealment, not defence.

### Opening the Hatch

- **Investigation DC 11:** Finds the recessed lifting ring.
- **Strength DC 13:** Raises the hatch without finding the release catch.
- **Investigation DC 13:** Finds the concealed counterweight release beside the brass border.
- **Using the release:** Allows one person to raise the hatch easily.
- **Forcing or breaking the hatch:** Sends a vibration through all twelve pillars below, although there are no living Dawnmantles left to feel it.

## Read Aloud: Opening the Hatch

> Beneath the green carpet, the wooden floor gives way to a broad slab of dark stone. Twelve worn sunbursts encircle a recessed iron ring at its centre.
>
> The hatch lifts more easily than its size suggests.
>
> Beneath it, a narrow staircase descends into muted amber light. The first steps are pale and neatly cut, but the stone becomes darker and older as it falls away beneath the house.

# The Descending Stair

The staircase descends directly from the billiards room into the southern end of the Hall.

There is no wine-cellar route and no secondary entrance door.

The stair is narrow enough for one person to descend comfortably at a time.

Twenty-four steps lead downward. Every second riser bears one carved word:

1. Blood
2. Memory
3. Duty
4. Silence
5. Continuance
6. Sacrifice
7. Witness
8. Burden
9. Vigil
10. Threshold
11. Light
12. Dawn

A narrow golden line runs down the centre of the stairs and joins the patterned floor below.

The line is now dull and interrupted in several places by faint black staining.

As characters descend:

- Sound from the billiards room becomes muffled after the sixth step.
- The air grows warmer.
- Aasimar feel pressure beneath the collarbone.
- Vaelisar blood would once have caused the line to glow.
- Veinstone or black-purple residue causes the gold to darken further.

# Chamber Features

## 1. The Southern Arch

The staircase descends directly beneath a broad stone arch at the southern end of the Hall.

The outer face of the arch bears the house words:

> **The Dawn Is Carried, Not Chosen.**

The inner face, visible only after entering, bears an older inscription:

> **Twelve were appointed. Twelve must endure.**

There are no ceremonial doors between the staircase and the chamber.

## 2. The Processional Way

The central aisle runs from the southern entrance to the northern lectern.

Its floor consists of repeated geometric sunburst tiles divided into twelve ritual stages.

During an induction, the candidate advanced along the path while acknowledging each pillar.

Ancient gold channels run beneath the tiles, connecting:

- The twelve pillars.
- The two northern wells.
- The main lectern.
- The original first-light shaft.
- The wider Vaelisar seal-thread.

The gold is now dark and inert.

### Evidence of Later Interference

A careful search reveals:

- Newer blackened copper wire inserted beside the ancient gold.
- Fine holes drilled through the ceiling towards the manor above.
- Black-purple residue between the tiles.
- Sections of flooring that were lifted and replaced.
- Suppressive runes carved in a different hand.
- Thin crescent cuts scored through several sunbursts.

These alterations redirected the energy released by the deaths towards the black orb upstairs.

## 3. The Twelve Pillars

Six pillars stand along each side of the Processional Way.

The traditional circuit begins with the southwest pillar, continues north along the western row, crosses before the lectern and returns south along the eastern row.

Each pillar contains:

- The station's name.
- Its traditional inscription.
- The names of former occupants.
- The final current bearer named before the massacre.
- A sealed glass vial containing preserved blood.
- A hand-shaped recess used during induction.
- A gold channel linking it to the central ritual geometry.

The Concordance describes these vials as fluid, warm and responsive while their bearers live.

Every vial is now crystallised and blackened.

### General Checks

- **Investigation DC 12:** Identifies the most recently engraved name on each pillar.
- **Medicine DC 13:** Determines that the black crystal inside the vials was once blood.
- **Religion DC 14:** Recognises that the pillars represent ritual functions, not noble ranks.
- **Arcana DC 15:** Determines that the pillars were once bound to living creatures but no living connection remains.
- **Arcana or Religion DC 17:** Determines that all twelve connections ended within moments of one another.
- **Arcana or Religion DC 19:** Understands that the simultaneous severing released power that was deliberately redirected upward.

## 4. The Eight Ancestral Niches

Four arched niches line each side of the Hall.

Each contains an engraved tablet representing one period or branch of the family:

1. The Arkborne Line
2. The First Caer Oryn Line
3. The Ashen Dawn and the False Saints
4. The Compact Line
5. The Main Vaelisar House
6. The Vaelis Branch
7. The Hearthward Vaelisars
8. The Southgate Cadet Line

The first five tablets are prominently displayed.

The three cadet-branch tablets show signs that they were once hidden behind removable stone panels. The panels were removed when the main house became unable to maintain twelve holders without recalling distant and exiled relatives.

Behind the Vaelis tablet, someone scratched:

> We called them lesser until we needed their blood.

## 5. The Four Dawn Braziers

Four braziers burn within the larger side recesses.

They contain pale mineral discs rather than conventional fuel.

The flames represent:

- Inheritance
- Witness
- Sacrifice
- Return

They should not still be burning.

Their continued flame is residual power trapped within the chamber after the Eclipse Rite.

Current behaviour:

- The Inheritance flame burns low and steady.
- The Witness flame dims whenever someone speaks a lie in the Hall.
- The Sacrifice flame burns too strongly and occasionally bends downward into its own brazier.
- The Return flame flickers black at its centre.

Extinguishing the flames does not restore the Vaelisar binding.

## 6. The Northern Dais

A low carved boundary separates the main ritual floor from the northern sanctum.

Only inducted Dawnmantles, candidates and the Keeper of the Roll were traditionally permitted beyond it.

The dais contains:

- The Well of Remembered Blood.
- The Well of First Light.
- The main lectern.
- The ceremonial blade.
- The mounting point for the original sunburst clasp.
- The secret compartment holding the Concordance.

## 7. The Well of Remembered Blood

The western circular structure is a deep stone basin with a heavy lid.

Its water is perfectly still and appears black when viewed from above.

During induction, the patriarch and candidate each placed twelve drops of blood into the well.

The blood of compatible candidates formed thin golden threads across the surface.

The water now shows a dark film beneath its surface.

A character looking into it may see brief images of:

- Twelve people kneeling at their pillars.
- A black veil being drawn over the eastern well.
- A hand carving a crescent through a sunburst.
- A young man being restrained before the Twelfth Pillar.
- Blood flowing away from the pillars rather than towards them.
- A room above folding inward around a black point.

The visions are incomplete and silent.

Water removed from the well becomes ordinary clear water once taken beyond the dais.

## 8. The Well of First Light

The eastern circular structure contains a shallow disc of polished golden metal.

A narrow shaft once carried the first light of sunrise from the manor grounds into the Hall.

At the completion of a legitimate induction, the light struck the disc and travelled through the gold channels into all twelve pillars.

During the Eclipse Rite, the Sharites covered the disc with black glass and fitted copper focusing pieces around the shaft.

This inverted and redirected the gathered light.

Removing the dark glass and copper components weakens the connection feeding the black orb above, although it does not close the breach by itself.

## 9. The Main Lectern

The central lectern appears to contain only a shallow recess for the private genealogy.

Its top bears three carved symbols:

- A rising sun.
- An open hand.
- A closed eye.

The lectern is part of the original chamber construction and cannot be moved without breaking the ancient gold channels beneath it.

### The Secret Compartment

The hidden compartment opens when the symbols are pressed in this order:

1. Closed eye.
2. Open hand.
3. Rising sun.

The sequence means:

> Knowledge kept. Duty accepted. Dawn carried.

### Finding the Compartment

- **Investigation DC 13:** Notices thin seams around the lectern's front panel.
- **History DC 14:** Connects the three symbols to the old Vaelisar traditions.
- **Religion DC 15:** Determines the intended sequence.
- **Thieves' Tools DC 16:** Opens the compartment mechanically.
- **Speaking the house motto while touching the lectern:** Causes the correct symbols to warm in sequence.
- **A fully inducted Dawnmantle:** Would have opened it automatically. No such person remains alive.

Forcing the compartment triggers a radiant discharge. Creatures within five feet must make a **DC 14 Dexterity saving throw**, taking **2d6 radiant damage** on a failure or half on a success.

# The Concordance of Twelve Dawns

The hidden tome is broad, heavy and protected by dark brass corners.

Its pages are reinforced vellum. Twelve small golden sunbursts surround a larger black circle upon its cover.

Different sections were written by generations of holders of the Memory.

The tome was last updated before the massacre. It describes the twelve final bearers as living.

## Title Page

> **The Concordance of Twelve Dawns**
>
> **Being the names, stations, obligations and manner of continuation of those who carry the keeping of House Vaelisar.**
>
> **Let no name be removed because it brings shame.**
>
> **The house may reject the bearer. The burden does not.**

A recent addition reads:

> **The Twelve are restored.**
>
> **Let the Hall stand complete against the approaching dark.**

---

# Player-Readable Contents of the Concordance

The following sections can be read or summarised to the players without revealing the massacre directly.

## The Law of Twelve

> House Vaelisar shall preserve twelve living bearers.
>
> Each bearer shall hold one station.
>
> No station is greater than another, though the First Light shall speak for the gathered Twelve.
>
> A bearer may travel beyond the house and beyond the Crown. Distance does not unmake the bond.
>
> Exile does not unmake the bond.
>
> Disinheritance does not unmake the bond.
>
> Shame does not unmake the bond.
>
> Death alone opens a station.

A later annotation reads:

> Our ancestors mistook authority for necessity. The pillars require blood and compatibility, not obedience to the patriarch.

## Who May Be Inducted

The stated requirements are:

- Descent from Seraphel Vaelisar through a recorded family line.
- Recognised celestial manifestation.
- Completion of the twelve-day preparation.
- A vacant station.
- Acceptance by the pillar.
- Survival of the marking.
- Spoken consent before the gathered Hall.

The text confirms that direct descent from the reigning patriarch is not required.

It names the Vaelis, Hearthward and Southgate branches as legitimate sources of candidates.

A disputed note attributed to Caedren Vaelisar reads:

> The stones have never spoken of men.
>
> They have spoken only of blood.
>
> We may have mistaken the custom of our fathers for a law of the keeping.

This suggests that the family's male-only restriction may have been tradition rather than a requirement of the underlying seal.

## Preparation of a Candidate

The candidate remains within the manor for twelve days.

Each day he is brought before one pillar and taught its obligation.

During this period, the candidate must not:

- Leave the estate.
- Spill blood in anger.
- Deliberately invoke celestial power.
- Sleep outside the manor.
- Enter the Hall alone.
- Speak the complete oath.
- Touch a vacant pillar without an inducted witness.

Each evening, the Keeper asks:

> What do you inherit?

The candidate answers:

> That which was carried before me.

> What do you surrender?

> That which belongs only to me.

> What do you become?

> A bearer, not a master.

## Opening the Rite

The candidate enters from the south one hour before sunrise.

He is barefoot and wears a plain white garment open at the right shoulder.

The Dawnmantle is carried behind him.

The patriarch stands beside the First Pillar.

> **Patriarch:** Twelve were appointed.
>
> **Gathered bearers:** Twelve must endure.
>
> **Patriarch:** Blood has diminished.
>
> **Gathered bearers:** The dawn has not forgotten.

Absent bearers may be represented through the blood vial held within their pillar.

## The Circuit of the Pillars

The candidate begins with the First Light and walks clockwise around the Hall.

At each pillar, he places his right hand within the carved hollow.

The Keeper names the station and reads its obligation.

The candidate answers:

> I acknowledge the station.

The text warns:

> The candidate shall not be told which station is vacant.
>
> Ambition must not direct the choosing.

## The Naming of the Blood

After the circuit, the Keeper opens the private genealogy.

The candidate's ancestry is recited back to Seraphel Vaelisar.

Exiles and those struck from the public roll must also be named.

> **Keeper:** The house may cast out the name.
>
> **Gathered bearers:** The blood remains.

All surviving branches are then spoken aloud.

## The Blood and Light

The candidate kneels before the Well of Remembered Blood.

The patriarch cuts his palm with the ceremonial blade and contributes twelve drops.

The candidate then contributes twelve drops of his own.

If compatible, golden threads form between the offerings.

The candidate stands between the wells while the mantle is placed over his shoulders.

The mantle remains unclasped.

## The Carried Oath

> **Patriarch:** What stands above?
>
> **Candidate:** The house.
>
> **Patriarch:** What stands below?
>
> **Candidate:** The keeping.
>
> **Patriarch:** What binds the two?
>
> **Candidate:** The blood that carries dawn.
>
> **Patriarch:** Is the dawn yours?
>
> **Candidate:** It is not mine.
>
> **Patriarch:** Is the burden chosen?
>
> **Candidate:** It was chosen before me.
>
> **Patriarch:** Will you carry what you did not make?
>
> **Candidate:** I will carry it until blood, memory and light have failed.
>
> **Gathered bearers:** The station opens.
>
> **Candidate:** Then let it know me.

## The Choosing

The candidate stands upon the central tile.

The vacant pillar chooses him.

Its inscription illuminates and its hand recess opens.

The candidate places his bloodied hand inside.

The pillar closes around his wrist.

The text describes visions of:

- Distant aasimar bloodlines.
- Intersecting lines of celestial light.
- The Godscar.
- Immense pressure beneath the earth.
- Something pressing against the lattice.
- The awareness that the thing below has noticed a new bearer.

A note attributed to Caedren reads:

> It is not symbolic.
>
> The pressure is real.

## The Marking

The patriarch presses the original sunburst clasp beneath the candidate's right collarbone.

The chosen pillar releases gold-white light.

The Dawnmantle mark forms beneath the skin.

At the same moment:

- The candidate's blood vial fills inside the pillar.
- His name appears upon the stone.
- The pillar joins the other eleven.
- Every living Dawnmantle feels the new bearer.
- The mantle clasp closes.
- The wider Dawnline recognises the replacement.

When dawnlight strikes the eastern well, all twelve pillars illuminate.

> **Patriarch:** The dawn is carried.
>
> **New Dawnmantle:** It is not mine to keep.

## Replacement of a Dead Bearer

The Concordance states:

1. The dead bearer's pillar darkens.
2. The blood within the pillar becomes black and still.
3. The name remains upon the stone.
4. A new candidate must be selected from all recognised branches.
5. Replacement should occur within one year.
6. A longer vacancy transfers strain into the remaining eleven.
7. A vacancy lasting more than twelve years begins affecting unborn descendants.

The final warning is underlined:

> **Never allow the ring to remain incomplete when the keeping is under pressure.**

## The Appearance of Living and Dead Blood

The Concordance provides the clue needed to understand the chamber:

> The remembered blood of a living bearer shall remain fluid.
>
> It shall move when its bearer wakes, warm when he draws upon the dawn, and shine when the complete Twelve are gathered.
>
> If the bearer dies, the blood shall darken and become still.
>
> If death occurs while the station is under strain, the blood may harden as stone.

All twelve vials in the Hall have hardened into black crystal.

## Prohibitions

A full page lists actions that must never be taken:

> Do not connect the twelve pillars to a single vessel.
>
> Do not draw their light upward for the service of the house.
>
> Do not place foreign stone within the golden channels.
>
> Do not replace remembered blood with prepared blood.
>
> Do not permit a stranger to speak through the Voice.
>
> Do not use the Burden as a reservoir.
>
> Do not bind an object where a living station should stand.
>
> The Hall sustains the keeping. It was not constructed to contain what men bring into it.

The Sharites violated nearly every prohibition.

**Bearer:** Dariel Vaelisar II  
**Inscription:** What is kept must remain kept.

Sound becomes muted within several feet of the pillar.

The blood has become a smooth black crystal without bubbles or fractures.

This pillar produces no death echo.

Dariel is dead.

## Pillar XI: The Last Light

**Bearer:** Coriel Vaelisar

A tiny point of white light remains trapped inside the black blood crystal.

The station was designed to continue briefly after the failure of the other eleven.

Coriel is dead.

**Bearer:** Tarian Vaelisar  
**Inscription:** What is diminished may rise again.

Tarian's engraved name is visibly newer than the others.

The outer edge of the blood crystal remains faintly translucent, but the centre is black.

A second unfinished line was prepared beneath his name for a future successor.

Tarian is dead.

# The Sharite Eclipse Rite

The Vaelisar induction was inverted into a ritual killing.

The attackers used the family's own chamber, positions and words, but reversed the intended movement of blood and light.

## Physical Evidence

The party can find:

- Crescent cuts made through sunburst carvings.
- New blackened copper wire beside the ancient gold channels.
- Dark glass placed over the Well of First Light.
- Blood flow marks leading away from the pillars.
- Restraint marks at each ritual position.
- Additional blood outside the twelve central positions.
- Four braziers altered to draw their flames downward.
- Sharite script carved beneath the lectern.
- Black-purple grit in the channel leading towards the orb above.

## The Sharite Prayer

Carved beneath the lectern:

> Let every carried light be extinguished.
>
> Let their keeping become a door.
>
> Let the dawn consume itself.
>
> Let the world know night.

## How the Rite Worked

The Sharites arranged the twelve fully inducted bearers in their proper ritual stations.

They then killed them in a controlled sequence timed to sever all twelve links together.

The simultaneous collapse:

1. Destroyed the Vaelisar section of the old god's prison.
2. Released the celestial energy stored in twelve living bonds.
3. Redirected that energy through the altered floor channels.
4. Forced the energy upward into one convergence point.
5. Opened the black planar wound now floating upstairs.

The black orb is not an enchanted object in the ordinary sense.

It is the stable scar left by the successful destruction of the complete Dawnmantle circle.

# The Other Vaelisar Dead

The twelve Dawnmantles were not the only victims.

The Sharites also killed:

- Uninducted relatives.
- Potential future candidates.
- Spouses and family witnesses.
- Household attendants who had seen the gathered bearers.
- Anyone capable of identifying the alterations to the rite.

These deaths were intended to:

- Prevent immediate replacement of the twelve holders.
- Remove witnesses.
- Supply additional blood to the Eclipse Rite.
- Conceal the exact nature of the attack.
- Leave the surviving distant line too fragmented to restore the Hall quickly.

There may still be distant Vaelisar descendants elsewhere, but there are no living inducted Dawnmantles.

The chamber contains signs of additional victims around the outside of the central ritual geometry, though the twelve pillar positions are the most precise and deliberate.

# The Black Orb and the Old God

The black orb upstairs was created by the successful collapse of the complete Vaelisar binding.

It connects to another plane.

The aberrations emerging from it are not necessarily creatures deliberately chosen or controlled by the Sharites.

The imprisoned old god's corruption spreads through the damaged binding and contaminates the planar breach.

The result is:

- Suppressive and antimagic effects near the orb.
- Purple-black residue.
- Aberrations forced or drawn through the opening.
- Pressure upon other aasimar seal-lines.
- Increased strain upon the wider prison beneath Lazuril.

Destroying the orb by force may release the energy holding the wound open.

The safer method is to:

1. Remove or sever the newer blackened copper connections.
2. Remove the dark glass from the Well of First Light.
3. Break the Sharite crescent alterations without damaging the ancient gold channels.
4. Disconnect the upper containment apparatus from the Hall.
5. Collapse or stabilise the orb after it is no longer drawing power from the dead Vaelisar circle.

The Vaelisar seal-thread cannot be restored merely by closing the orb.

It will eventually require a new set of compatible descendants and a reconstructed Rite of the Carried Dawn.

# Discovery Structure

## What the Tome Initially Suggests

From the Concordance alone, the players should conclude:

- Twelve Dawnmantles existed.
- They were living when the final entries were written.
- Tarian recently completed the circle.
- All twelve gathered at House Vaelisar.
- They intended to reinforce an ancient keeping.
- The rite was part of something larger than noble ceremony.
- Several holders came from exiled or cadet branches.

## What the Chamber Reveals

Physical examination can establish:

- The blood in every vial has crystallised.
- The tome says crystallisation occurs when a bearer dies under ritual strain.
- None of the pillars remains linked to a living creature.
- All twelve bonds ended almost simultaneously.
- The ritual channels were altered before the deaths.
- Released energy was redirected upwards.
- The black orb was created through the death of the complete circle.

## What Requires Deeper Investigation

The party needs stronger checks or specific evidence to learn:

- The attackers were Sharites.
- They are attempting to extinguish the aasimar seal-houses.
- The Vaelisar and Amakiir attacks were parts of the same plan.
- Cael Tharos failed to form an orb because Alekthar's sister was not fully inducted.
- The old god's corruption is responsible for the aberrations.
- If every aasimar binding house fails, the old god will be released.

The room is warm, but the pillars are cold.

All twelve vials have hardened.

## Third Reveal: They Died Together

Arcane and religious examination shows that the bonds ended at almost the same moment.

## Fourth Reveal: The Rite Was Inverted

The crescent cuts, black glass, copper channels and Sharite prayer show deliberate sabotage.

## Fifth Reveal: The Orb Is the Result

The altered channels lead upward towards the black orb.

The released power of the twelve deaths created the planar wound.

## Sixth Reveal: This Is Part of a Wider Attack

The structure matches what happened at Cael Tharos, except that the incomplete induction there prevented a stable orb from forming.

## Final Campaign Reveal

The aasimar families collectively bind the old god beneath Lazuril.

The Sharites are eliminating those families to bring lasting darkness over the world and release what lies below.
