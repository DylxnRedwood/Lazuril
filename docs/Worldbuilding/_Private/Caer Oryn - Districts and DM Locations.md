# Caer Oryn: Districts and DM Locations

> [!dm] DM-Only City Reference
> This file expands the Caer Oryn district structure described in [[The Vermillion Crown]] for private campaign use. It is written for running scenes, travel, vendors, access checks, district pressure, and location descriptions during *The Bindings Must Be Remade*.
>
> Related files: [[Session 2 - Ashes at the Last Lantern]], [[Session 2 - NPCs and Locations]], [[Morfael - NPCs and Sites]], [[Morfael - Shops and Vendors]].

## City Summary

Caer Oryn is the capital of [[The Vermillion Crown]], built along the River Oryn and rising toward the Hillkeep. It is rich, organised, watched, and useful. It should not feel like a chaotic medieval sprawl. Even its poorer districts are registered, serviced, inspected, patrolled, and made economically productive.

The city’s danger is not filth or anarchy. Its danger is order.

The lower districts contain the labour, docks, warehouses, workshops, modest housing, customs houses, and river mechanisms that keep the capital alive. Higher districts climb into guild power, education, ministry, military administration, and the Lord Protector’s seat.

## Running Caer Oryn

> [!dm] Tone
> Caer Oryn should feel prosperous, disciplined, surveilled, and self-assured. People do not whisper because they are poor. They whisper because the city writes things down.
>
> Soldiers are polite. Clerks are dangerous. Gates are clean. Permits matter. Bells and ledgers move people as much as swords do.

### General Access Rules

| Access Level | Who Has It | What It Allows |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Residents, licensed travellers, day labourers, pilgrims, market traffic | Streets, public markets, temples, outer quays, common taverns, basic offices. |
| Registered | Residents, guild members, students, temple staff, permitted traders | District offices, guild counters, lower archives, civic schools, inner markets. |
| Licensed | Approved merchants, chartered carriers, military suppliers, temple officials | Warehouses, toll registries, controlled cargo routes, private guild offices. |
| Writ-Bearing | Directorate officers, approved investigators, senior clergy, court agents | Restricted district offices, sealed records, controlled gates, official escorts. |
| Crown-Restricted | Civic Directorate, military command, state security, named specialists | Foundry yards, state vaults, Hillkeep offices, military archives, hidden infrastructure. |

### Cadris’ Day Security

Cadris’ Day is the public celebration of Maeron Cadris’ seizure of power and the official story of the state’s renewal. In Caer Oryn it produces closures, temporary barriers, route changes, procession permits, inspection schedules, and heightened patrols.

During Cadris’ Day preparations:

- The Foundry Quarter is under temporary Crown Writ Control.
- Marrowgate Road, Harrow Road Landing, and river-chain crossings are checked more heavily.
- Cargo licences are reviewed against old ledgers.
- Pilgrims are redirected through approved streets.
- The Final Journey receives increased requests for state-sanctioned rites, procession dead, military memorials, and pauper removals.
- Anyone asking about Edrin Vaal, red dust, forged cargo marks, or Magpie Lane attracts attention faster than usual.

## The River Districts

> [!dm] Core Identity
> The River Districts are the working throat of Caer Oryn: docks, warehouses, labour halls, river stairs, chain gates, modest housing, river shrines, customs offices, lock towers, taverns, ropewalks, net yards, ferry ledgers, and cheap lodging.
>
> This is where Morfael connects to Caer Oryn in practical terms. The River Districts are cleaner and more controlled than Morfael, but the same water runs through both.

> [!readaloud] Read Aloud: River Districts
> The city begins at the river with stone, chain, and shouting.
>
> Wide quays step down into the brown-green water of the Oryn. Iron rings line the dock walls. Chain gates hang over the current like the teeth of some sleeping machine. Barges wait under painted numbers while clerks move between them with wax tablets and slate boards. Dockhands lift, curse, laugh, and fall silent whenever a Crown officer passes too close.
>
> Above the riverfront, narrow streets climb between warehouses, shrines, rope stores, bakeries, labour offices, cheap taverns, and whitewashed civic posts. Nothing here looks abandoned. Even the cracks have been counted.

### Access

Public access is possible in most outer streets during ordinary days. Warehouses, customs posts, river-chain mechanisms, lock towers, and state cargo lanes require licence, labour token, writ, or escort.

During Cadris’ Day, even public access becomes channelled. Travellers are pushed through approved market routes and away from chain gates, military cargo quays, and procession staging yards.

### What Happens Here

- Cargo is unloaded, inspected, weighed, stamped, taxed, and redirected.
- River labourers register for day work and state contracts.
- Poorer families live in upper rooms above warehouses, shops, and labour offices.
- Temples serve the dead, travellers, boatmen, soldiers, and workers.
- The Passage Directorate controls locks, chain gates, ferry schedules, and water movement.
- Smugglers operate here by imitating normal paperwork, not by hiding in shadows.

## The Final Journey Church

> [!dm] Placement
> The Final Journey has its main Caer Oryn church in the River Districts, not the High Quarter. This is deliberate. The faith serves the dead where the dead arrive: river, road, accident yard, dockside, hospital cart, soldier’s bier, pauper wagon.
>
> The High Quarter has formal memorial chapels and noble crypt rights, but the River District church does the city’s real death work.

### The Church of the Final Journey

The main church is called **The House of the Last Road** by clergy, but most locals call it **The Final Journey**.

It stands on a raised stone terrace above the confluence of three river streets. Its lower doors open toward the corpse carts and pauper wagons. Its upper doors face a processional stair used for soldiers, citizens of note, and state-sanctioned funerals.

> [!readaloud] Read Aloud: The Final Journey
> The church rises from the river district like a toll gate for the dead.
>
> Its stone is pale but weather-stained, darkened near the lower doors by decades of wet wheels, boot mud, and funeral carts. Iron lanterns hang from long brackets along the outer wall, each one hooded against river wind. Above the main arch is carved a road vanishing into a horizon, with a single lantern set beside it.
>
> The lower entrance is plain, broad, and practical. The upper entrance is formal, with steps worn smooth by processions. Between them, the building seems to admit what the city pretends not to know: death comes through every door, but not everyone is carried through the same one.

### Interior

> [!readaloud] Read Aloud: Interior
> Inside, the air is cool and mineral. Rows of low lanterns burn along the walls rather than above the congregation, so the light stays near the floor and catches on shoes, hems, bier wheels, and the edges of shrouds.
>
> The nave is long and undecorated except for names. Names carved into side tablets. Names written on slate. Names hanging from thin cord beside unclaimed ashes. At the far end, there is no triumphant statue, only a stone threshold and a dark arch painted with a road beneath stars.

### Access

| Area | Access |
|---|---|
| Public nave | Open to mourners, travellers, poor families, and petitioners. |
| Mortuary hall | Clergy, body-washers, temple assistants, approved witnesses. |
| Record room | Clergy, Scriptor staff, senior temple scribes, authorised investigators. |
| Processional crypt | Noble families, military officials, senior clergy, named permit only. |
| Lower ash vault | Restricted. Used for unclaimed dead, plague concerns, state bodies, and disputed remains. |

### Key NPCs

#### Arch-Lector Meren Voss

High-ranking priest of The Final Journey in Caer Oryn. Voss is Teclis’ superior contact. He is not soft, but he is not stupid. He measures danger by how quickly the powerful want a corpse moved.

**Use:** delayed instructions, ecclesiastical writs, theological framing, controlled access to church records.

**Line:**

> “A state that fears a body will always call it property before it calls it a soul.”

#### Brother Halrec Thorne

Human man, 50s. Mortuary master.

Halrec oversees washing halls, body intake, disputed remains, and transport ledgers. He dislikes drama and can identify whether a body was treated by battlefield medics, civic surgeons, or experimental handlers.

**Use:** forensic clue source, practical death work, a bridge between Halva and Voss.

#### Scriptor Eloan Vey

Full elf, age 180. Temple Scriptor assigned to death records.

Eloan is one of the full elves permitted under Compact law to serve as Scriptor. They are precise, quiet, and deeply irritated by sloppy mortal record-keeping.

**Use:** controlled record access, Teclis professional tension, Compact lore.

- During Cadris’ Day, the church is overloaded with ceremonial duties, giving both cover and delay.

## The Guild Quarter

> [!dm] Core Identity
> The Guild Quarter is where commerce becomes law. Merchant houses, banking halls, guild courts, toll registries, caravan offices, trade brokers, contract readers, and licensed intermediaries operate here.
>
> It is not a street market. It is a machine for turning permission into money.

> [!readaloud] Read Aloud: Guild Quarter
> The Guild Quarter smells of paper, wax, perfume, and polished stone.
>
> The streets are wide enough for carriages but narrow enough to make every doorway feel expensive. Brass plaques cover the walls beside counting houses and charter offices. Runners in coloured sashes move between buildings with sealed tubes at their belts. Armed guards stand outside banks with the relaxed posture of people paid well enough not to look nervous.
>
> Here, no one asks what you want. They ask who authorises you to want it.

### Access

Public streets are open. Guild halls, banking rooms, contract courts, and charter archives require appointment, membership, sponsorship, legal standing, or payment.

During Cadris’ Day, the Guild Quarter is busy with processional contracts, state catering, military supply ledgers, visiting merchants, and licence reviews.

- A forged Foundry Quarter work token can be bought, but it is expensive and flawed.
- Guild officials can slow the party with procedure rather than force.

## The Academy Quarter

> [!dm] Core Identity
> The Academy Quarter contains schools, colleges, libraries, officer academies, legal halls, map archives, magical institutes, and state scholarship houses.
>
> It exists because the Crown believes knowledge is useful when registered, funded, and directed.

> [!readaloud] Read Aloud: Academy Quarter
> The Academy Quarter rises in terraces of pale stone, slate roofs, glass lanterns, and strict courtyards.
>
> Students in dark coats hurry under covered walks. Officers drill in mathematics beside weapon yards. Legal apprentices recite statutes from memory. Mages in licensed collars carry copper tubes of notes between locked buildings. Every archway bears a motto about service, order, or the proper use of knowledge.
>
> Even curiosity seems to require permission here.

### Access

Public lectures and outer libraries may be open with visitor tokens. Inner archives, magical institutes, observatories, legal halls, and officer schools require credentials, invitation, student status, Compact connection, or Directorate writ.

## The Foundry Quarter

> [!dm] Core Identity
> The Foundry Quarter is controlled industry: armouries, inspection yards, military workshops, regulated foundries, river-chain mechanisms, state weapon houses, material testing halls, and secure labour barracks.
>
> This is not a place of free artisans. It is a state machine.

> [!readaloud] Read Aloud: Foundry Quarter Exterior
> You hear the Foundry Quarter before you see it.
>
> Hammerfalls roll through the streets in dull, disciplined rhythm. Soot hangs above the roofs in a low brown veil. Tall brick stacks, iron gantries, enclosed yards, and high-walled workshops crowd together behind controlled gates. Every entrance has numbers. Every cart has a tag. Every worker shows a token before passing inside.
>
> The smell is coal smoke, hot metal, river damp, oil, and something sharper beneath it, like lightning trapped in stone.

### Access

The Foundry Quarter is heavily restricted even on ordinary days.

During Cadris’ Day preparations, it is under **temporary Crown Writ Control**. No casual entry.

| Access Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| Labour token | Must match shift, workshop, and gate. Forgery possible but risky. |
| Cargo licence | Must match freight, weight, origin, destination, and seal. |
| Ecclesiastical writ | Possible for death inquiry, workplace fatality, or disputed remains. Voss can provide later. |
| Watch escort | Must be authorised above Strake. Local watch alone is not enough. |
| Crown pass | Cleanest official entry; most dangerous if false. |
| Inside contact | Best route for stealth/social play. Creates obligation. |

### Vendors and Fronts

There are few true public shops inside the Foundry Quarter. Most “vendors” are contract desks, supply windows, or controlled counters.

| Name | Type | Proprietor | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gate Seven Token Desk | Labour check-in | Clerk Hadrin Voss | Work tokens, shift ledgers, gate arguments. |
| Ironmeasure Hall | Material inspection office | Inspector Sera Marn | Weight discrepancies, red dust residue, false cargo. |
| Grey Tag Window | State freight counter | Unknown rotating clerks | Blank grey tags, bypassed inspection, Crown freight. |
| Magpie Lane Canteen | Worker canteen | Olla Breck | Worker rumours, injuries, missing shifts. |
| Blackwater Gearhouse | Industrial tools | Foreman Kel Rusk | Hooks, masks, gloves, chain tools, suspicious restraints. |
| The West Furnace Steps | Informal smoking break spot | No owner | Worker gossip, bribes, fear. |

### Magpie Lane

Magpie Lane is a narrow service lane inside the Foundry Quarter used by clerks, runners, canteen staff, and small cargo carts. It should not look like the heart of the conspiracy. It should look almost disappointingly ordinary.

> [!readaloud] Read Aloud: Magpie Lane
> Magpie Lane is narrow, hot, and practical. Steam leaks from a wall pipe. Someone has chalked shift numbers onto a black door. Empty lunch tins sit in a crate beside a canteen hatch. The stones are cracked by cart wheels and stained with oil.
>
> Nothing here announces itself as important.
>
> That is what makes the locked side door stand out.

### DM Hooks

- Edrin’s route points toward Magpie Lane, but not directly to the hidden facility.
- The party may first find missing shift records, medical supply orders, or grey-tagged freight.
- Workers know about injuries, not conspiracies.
- The Crown Scar Directorate should remain hidden beneath layers of normal state industry.
- A successful entry should reveal a rung, not the ladder.

## The High Quarter

> [!dm] Core Identity
> The High Quarter is wealth under discipline: ministerial estates, noble homes, embassies, treasury halls, high courts, theatres, civic temples, formal gardens, private tutors, licensed salons, and houses that know how to be elegant without looking uncontrolled.

> [!readaloud] Read Aloud: High Quarter
> The streets widen as the city climbs.
>
> River noise fades behind clipped hedges, pale walls, polished gates, and carriage wheels over clean stone. The houses here do not shout wealth. They state it with ironwork, private guards, imported trees, and windows placed to overlook those below. Courtyards hold fountains. Statues hold scrolls, swords, and lamps. Servants move quickly and quietly.
>
> Even the flowers look inspected.

### Access

Public access is limited to main roads, temples, theatres, legal courts, and formal gardens. Private estates, ministerial offices, treasury halls, and embassy houses require invitation, appointment, escort, or title.

## The Hillkeep

> [!dm] Core Identity
> The Hillkeep is the seat of Maeron Cadris and the Civic Directorate. It contains the Lord Protector’s keep, command chambers, state vaults, national archives, high courts, military councils, and central directorate offices.
>
> It should not be accessible early. It should loom.

> [!readaloud] Read Aloud: Hillkeep from Below
> Above the city, the Hillkeep watches.
>
> Its walls are pale against the sky, too high to seem part of the streets below. Banners hang without fluttering in the still morning air. Towers catch the sun before the river districts do. From a distance, the keep looks less like a castle than a verdict rendered in stone.
>
> Every road in Caer Oryn seems to remember it is there.

### Access

Almost none without official reason. Petitioners can reach lower courts and public registry halls by appointment. Everything meaningful requires writ, rank, summons, or arrest.

### Offices and Restricted Areas

| Name | Type | Use |
|---|---|---|
| The Civic Directorate Hall | Central administration | Movement law, state authority, permits, orders. |
| The Passage Office High Registry | Movement and route control | Godscar passage, bridge records, river authority. |
| The Armature Directorate Annex | Military supply command | Foundry orders, weapons, state material flow. |
| The Surveyor Vaults | Maps and land records | Hidden routes, old works, censored surveys. |
| The State Archive | National records | Coup history, old noble ledgers, sealed deaths. |
| The Lord Protector’s Keep | Seat of Maeron Cadris | Do not use casually. Campaign-late gravity. |

### DM Hooks

- Early references to Hillkeep should be documents, seals, commands, and fear.
- Hillkeep officials may affect the players long before they meet any.
- A denial from Hillkeep is more frightening than a threat from a thug.
- Cadris’ Day ties the whole city’s movement to the Lord Protector’s image.
