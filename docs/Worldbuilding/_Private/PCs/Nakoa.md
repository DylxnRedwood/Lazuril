## Nakoa

_Private working page for Nakoa. This page is intended for campaign development, DM reference, future session notes, hidden character continuity, and long-term tracking of Nakoa's connection to Ithrava, the Veylrath Clans, and the black-and-gold feline relic._

Nakoa, often called **Nak** by those close to him, is a Tabaxi Arcane Trickster Rogue of the **Veylrath Clans**, born in the dense rainforest reaches of north-eastern Ithrava. To human settlers, scholars, and the younger political powers of the continent, this land is widely known as **Arkhaven**. To the Tabaxi and many older peoples, it has long been called **Ithrava**, meaning **The Land That Breathes**. Among the Veylrath, that name is not ornamental. It is a statement of lived truth. The land breathes through river mist, rain-heavy leaves, moss, animal movement, floodwater, overgrown stone, and the patient return of root and vine over anything foolish enough to believe itself permanent.

Nakoa begins the campaign as someone shaped by three forces: the living rainforest world of his birth, the disciplined law and memory of the Veylrath Clans, and a dangerous private mystery he does not yet understand. He is curious, charming, restless, and difficult to keep out of places he should avoid. He has been taught caution by people who knew better than him, but he has not yet learned to obey caution when mystery stands in front of him.

That contradiction is the centre of the character.

Nakoa is not reckless because he is stupid. He is reckless because he believes the unknown is a door, and because some part of him has always suspected that the world hides its most important truths behind warnings written by frightened people.

## Origin: Ithrava and the Veylrain Expanse

Nakoa was born in the **Veylrain Expanse**, a vast rainforest region of sprawling rivers, ancient trees, buried ruins, dangerous beasts, wetland routes, canopy settlements, and old stone reclaimed by the jungle. The Expanse is not a wilderness in the simple human sense. It is inhabited, travelled, remembered, guarded, argued over, and named in ways outsiders rarely understand.

To the Veylrath, routes matter as much as walls. A settlement is not only the place where people sleep. It is the river bend that floods safely, the platform that survives storms, the rope bridge replaced every season, the tree that must not be cut, the ruin whose threshold is watched, the pool where certain medicines grow, and the path whose name changes depending on the month.

Nakoa's home settlement is **Tethvale**, a river-trade and navigation hub raised above the bends of the Saelun River. By human city standards, Tethvale would be considered small, lacking stone districts, paved avenues, and formal walls. By Tabaxi and Veylrath standards, it is large, lively, and important. It is a place of suspended platforms, rope bridges, woven lifts, river stairs, watch-nests, ferry lines, trade families, storytellers, river-speakers, route keepers, and map-keepers who understand that a route through the jungle is never just a line between two places.

Tethvale connects several important nearby settlements. To the north-east lies **Kelshara**, a quieter place of historians, relic preservationists, ruin scholars, restoration workers, and moss-covered terraces. Kelshara is unusually open to serious human scholars, provided they work under recognised host protection, clan law, and strict scholar agreements. To the south-west stands **Vurokai**, where the rainforest thins toward savannah and open scrub. Its people are hunters, trackers, monster slayers, scouts, bowyers, and hide-workers who protect nearby settlements from predators and older things that wander from ruined ground after heavy rain. Further downriver from Tethvale, near the Lo-Varin Pools, sits **Nareth Lo**, a settlement of healers, alchemists, fungal gardens, rare jungle plants, poisons, antidotes, and quiet practical expertise.

Nakoa grew up inside this network, not outside it. His later wandering should not be treated as a rejection of home. It is more accurate to say that home taught him the world was full of routes, and he took that lesson too seriously.

## Family

Nakoa is the second in a litter of five.

His elder brother, [Kaelo](Kaelo.md), remained in Tethvale to help their father manage the family's trade routes and local dealings between nearby settlements. Kaelo represents duty, continuity, and the version of Nakoa's life that would have made sense to almost everyone.

The younger twins, [Suri](Suri.md) and [Riven](Riven.md), became renowned local monster trackers. They work as an inseparable pair through rainforest trails, river margins, and savannah borderlands. Their competence gives the family local respect beyond trade.

The youngest sibling, [Enzo](Enzo.md), fancies himself a great bard and storyteller. He is never happier than when exaggerating the exploits of his siblings to anyone willing to listen, especially when those siblings are not present to correct him.

Their father, [Pathspeaker Tarek](Pathspeaker%20Tarek.md), is a respected trader, navigator, and negotiator responsible for maintaining safe travel routes through dangerous rainforest waterways. He knows which river bends shift after heavy rain, which canopy paths survive flood season, which families honour passage, which routes need gifts, which routes need silence, and which old sites are better avoided entirely.

Their mother, [Amara](Amara.md), was once a relic appraiser and historian before settling into family life. Warm, sharp-eyed, and almost impossible to fool, she encouraged each of her children to become what they truly were rather than what family or clan expectation might find convenient.

Nakoa's family should matter in play because they anchor him. He is not a lone stray with no obligations. He has people who know him, love him, worry about him, and have every right to ask what exactly he thought he was doing in an old ruin with his hands on a cursed-looking object.

## Markings and Appearance

Like other Tabaxi of the Veylrath Clans, Nakoa bears distinctive family markings within his fur. These are not surnames, tattoos, or decorative clan brands. They are living inheritance: natural patterns formed from the merging of parental bloodlines and read as indicators of ancestry, kinship, inheritance, marriage ties, and clan belonging.

Nakoa's markings curl across his shoulders, upper back, and arms in winding patterns that resemble jungle vines, flowing rivers, fragmented sunbursts, and old map-lines. Unlike the cleaner symmetry carried by some Tabaxi, his markings twist and branch unpredictably, as though following overgrown paths through places not meant to be found.

Combined with his Bengal-like coat of rich golden fur, dark rosettes, and jungle-black striping, the markings make him memorable even before his personality does. More than one elder has joked that his fur looks less like natural patterning and more like the jungle trying to write something.

Since the relic incident, faint golden flecks have begun appearing in those markings. They are most visible across his shoulders, arms, and back, and they shimmer most strongly when he unknowingly draws upon the Weave. This should be treated as a subtle but important visual tell. Nakoa may be able to hide it under clothing or lighting, but anyone who knows Veylrath markings well could recognise that something about him has changed.

## Personality

Nakoa is curious, quick, expressive, and difficult to keep still when a mystery is close enough to smell.

He is not cruel, and he is not driven by greed in the conventional thiefly sense. He likes clever objects, old stories, forbidden routes, lost mechanisms, hidden doors, relics, maps, rumoured chambers, and the specific type of locked container that makes adults lower their voices. He enjoys risk partly because risk makes the world feel awake.

He can be charming without being polished. He apologises easily, but the apology often arrives after the damage is already done. He is capable of guilt, especially where his family or clan law is concerned, but guilt does not always stop him from wanting answers.

At his best, Nakoa is brave, perceptive, loyal, and imaginative. He sees possibilities where others see warnings. He notices odd details and makes connections quickly. He can treat ruins not as piles of treasure, but as unfinished conversations with the dead.

At his worst, he confuses access with entitlement. He hears a warning and mistakes it for a challenge. He trusts cleverness too much. He can tell himself that he is seeking understanding when part of him is also chasing the thrill of being the one who finds what others missed.

## Arden Rook

[Arden Rook](Arden%20Rook.md) is one of the defining figures in Nakoa's life.

When Nakoa was young, Arden arrived near Tethvale carrying locked cases, map rolls, sealed parcels, and the sort of careful silence that attracts the attention of every curious child in a settlement. Rumours followed him immediately. Some said he had come from Kelshara. Others said he had travelled far beyond the Expanse. A few claimed he possessed relics, forbidden maps, and magical curiosities gathered from across Ithrava.

Nakoa attempted to sneak into Arden's temporary dwelling and was caught almost immediately.

Rather than punish him, Arden recognised something familiar: boldness, intelligence, restlessness, and the fatal inability to leave a mystery alone. Over time, Arden became an unlikely mentor. He taught Nakoa how to move unseen, how to open locks without force, how to read people as carefully as maps, how to listen for what was not being said, and how to survive when cleverness had already failed.

Arden's hidden history matters. To most people he is a travelling scholar with a tired face, dry humour, and a habit of noticing exits before introductions. In truth, he was once an operative of **The Veiled Path**, a continent-spanning hidden network often dismissed as a thieves' guild. That reputation is too simple. The Veiled Path moves stolen goods, relics, letters, prisoners, poisons, names, maps, forbidden books, forged permits, and secrets through safehouses, coded routes, criminal brokers, dockside runners, and false identities. Most people think they are thieves. Most thieves hope that is all they are.

Arden left that world after uncovering something in the ancient ruins of Ithrava. He has never fully explained what he found. Whatever it was, it made him quieter, more cautious, and far less amused by people who treat old places as treasure vaults.

The key lesson Arden tried to teach Nakoa was not simply how to steal. It was when not to reach.

Nakoa did not always listen.

## Talu and Quiet Carrying

[Talu](Talu.md) is a Tabaxi courier and childhood friend of Nakoa. Among outsiders, Talu might easily be called a smuggler, and he does very little to discourage the romance of that reputation. Within Veylrath law, however, his work is more properly understood as **quiet carrying**.

Quiet carrying is the clan-recognised movement of goods, letters, medicines, warnings, and sometimes people without announcing every burden to foreign traders, rival families, patrols, or hostile powers along the route. It is not lawless. It is legal only while it serves clan law. Cargo may be hidden from outsiders, but not from the Canopy Concord, relevant elders, or ruin-wardens when relics and dangerous goods are involved.

Talu understands that line. He simply enjoys walking as close to it as possible.

For Nakoa, Talu is useful because he represents the blurred edge between cleverness and crime inside a lawful culture. He can provide routes, rumours, transport, favours, and trouble. He is also someone who knew Nakoa before the relic, which makes him valuable as a witness to what has changed.

## Seraya Venn

[Seraya Venn](Seraya%20Venn.md) is a human relic scholar and historian from Kelshara. She lives under scholar agreement and clan protection, having earned more trust than most outsiders through careful work, patience, and respect for Veylrath law.

Seraya encouraged Nakoa's fascination with ancient history and taught him the basics of identifying, handling, and preserving artifacts. From her, he learned that relics are never simply old objects. They are records, warnings, debts, wounds, or doors. Sometimes they are all of those things at once.

Seraya is important because she gives Nakoa's curiosity a scholarly dimension. Without her, his interest in relics could look like treasure-hunting. With her influence, it becomes more complicated. Nakoa has been taught enough to know better. He understands that relics should be appraised, recorded, protected, and brought before the right authorities.

That means when he touched the black-and-gold feline relic, he did not do so from ignorance.

He did it despite knowledge.

## The Relic Incident

During an expedition deep within overgrown ruins hidden beneath enormous strangler trees and thick hanging moss, Nakoa discovered a small relic unlike anything he had seen before. It resembled a crouched feline figure carved from smooth black stone, etched with thin golden markings that shimmered faintly in the dark.

Even before touching it, he felt a vibration beneath his fingertips, almost as though the object itself was breathing.

He knew, because Arden had taught him well, that he should not touch it.

He touched it anyway.

The moment his hand made contact, the relic cracked apart into a cloud of golden dust, bright and wild as a lion's mane. The particles spiralled around him before rushing into his lungs and eyes. His vision blurred. The world became distant and weightless, as though reality had loosened its grip. He remembers whispers in a language he did not understand, movement where nothing stood, and the feeling of something ancient turning its attention toward him.

Then he collapsed.

When he awoke hours later, the relic was gone.

## Emerging Magic

At first, nothing seemed different. Then small things began happening.

Objects shifted when Nakoa was not touching them. Locked mechanisms clicked open on their own. Cards slid across tables toward his fingers. Coins spun unnaturally when resting near his hands. In moments of stress or distraction, translucent claws or ghostly hands flickered into being before vanishing again.

This is the origin point of Nakoa's Arcane Trickster abilities, but it should not be framed as normal wizardry or simple innate talent. His magic feels like an intrusion, a residue, or an awakening caused by contact with something old in the Weave. It is nimble, furtive, tactile, and strangely best suited to the exact habits he already possessed: reaching, unlocking, distracting, misdirecting, and taking hold of what should be out of reach.

That may be coincidence.

It probably is not.

The golden flecks now appearing in Nakoa's markings suggest that the relic did not merely grant him a skill. It altered something in his body, ancestry, spirit, or connection to Ithrava itself. Whether it chose him, marked him, cursed him, infected him, or broke open something already waiting beneath his skin remains unknown.

## Clan Law and the Problem of What He Did

The Veylrath teach that curiosity must walk beside caution. Ruins are studied, guarded, and respected. Relics are brought before appraisers, historians, shamans, and ruin-wardens before being kept, moved, traded, sealed, or destroyed.

Nakoa knows these laws.

He knows them well enough to understand exactly how many of them he may have broken.

This gives him a private pressure point. His story should not be only about discovering powers. It should also be about responsibility. The more he learns about the relic, the harder it may become to pretend the incident was merely a mistake. If the relic was tied to early Godscar workings, ancient binding mechanisms, or older Veylrath ruin traditions, then Nakoa may not simply be a curious rogue with new magic. He may be a living breach in a system someone once built to keep something sealed.

## Role in Session 1

At campaign start, Nakoa should arrive as someone who can plausibly be drawn into trouble through curiosity, contacts, unusual relic knowledge, quiet movement, or private interest in strange magical evidence. He does not need to be forced toward the campaign premise. A dead body, a strange stone residue, a sealed object, a hidden route, a witness who will not speak publicly, or rumours of veinstone experimentation would all naturally draw him in.

Nakoa is useful to the party because he can move where others cannot, listen where others are noticed, read the value of objects without reducing them to coin, and recognise when a place is old in the wrong way.

He is dangerous to the party because he may reach first and think second.

## DM Use

Nakoa works best when temptation and warning appear together.

Do not make every warning false, or he becomes correct to ignore them. Do not make every warning fatal, or he becomes foolish for acting at all. The strongest Nakoa scenes should make him choose between knowledge and trust, curiosity and restraint, loyalty and secrecy, speed and proper ritual.

His personal arc can connect strongly to the campaign's central themes: ancient bindings, veinstone, the Godscar, hidden histories, inherited duties, and the difference between studying a seal and weakening it.

The black-and-gold feline relic should remain an open campaign thread. It may have been a key, a ward, an archive, a vessel, a test, a fragment of a larger mechanism, or something that had been waiting for a Tabaxi marked by a particular bloodline. Its feline form matters. Its golden dust matters. Its breathing quality matters. The fact that it vanished into Nakoa rather than remaining as an item matters most of all.

## Character Levers

Nakoa can be challenged through his family, especially by forcing him to confront how his choices affect [Kaelo](Kaelo.md), [Suri](Suri.md), [Riven](Riven.md), [Enzo](Enzo.md), [Pathspeaker Tarek](Pathspeaker%20Tarek.md), and [Amara](Amara.md).

He can be challenged through [Arden Rook](Arden%20Rook.md), whose past in The Veiled Path and fear of old ruins may make him both protector and obstacle.

He can be challenged through [Talu](Talu.md), whose quiet carrying may place him near dangerous information, illegal goods, or morally ambiguous routes.

He can be challenged through [Seraya Venn](Seraya%20Venn.md), who may be sympathetic to Nakoa's curiosity but far less forgiving of relic mishandling than he expects.

He can be challenged through Veylrath law, where affection does not erase responsibility and where curiosity without caution can endanger more than the curious person.

## Open Questions

What exactly was the black-and-gold feline relic?

Was it connected to early Godscar veinstone workings, Veylrath ruin-warding, a forgotten Tabaxi lineage, or something older than the clans?

Did it choose Nakoa specifically, or would it have altered anyone who touched it?

Are the golden flecks in his markings a sign of power, corruption, inheritance, or a claim of ownership?

Does Arden Rook know more about similar relics than he has admitted?

Would Seraya Venn report the incident if she knew the full truth?

Would Pathspeaker Tarek protect Nakoa, surrender him to the proper ruin-wardens, or try to do both?

What happens if the ancient thing that noticed Nakoa turns its attention toward him again?
