# The Gilded Hart Hotel

_Private DM-facing location for Caer Oryn._

## Purpose at the Table

The Gilded Hart is a suitable place for the party to stay while operating in the High Quarter of [[Caer Oryn]]. It sits close enough to [[House Vaelisar]] that the party can use it as a base for surveillance, rumour-gathering, late-night movement, and uncomfortable conversations with people who know more than they admit.

It is elegant, lively, expensive, and difficult to disappear inside unless the characters understand upper-quarter manners. The hotel is not hostile by default, but it is observant. Servants remember faces. Doormen notice weapons. Clerks record names. The wealthy pretend not to stare, then talk about strangers over wine.

Use the Gilded Hart when the party needs:

- A refined place to rest without leaving the High Quarter.
- A base within walking distance of House Vaelisar.
- NPCs who have heard useful fragments about the Civic Directorate sealing the manor.
- A social pressure cooker where unusual characters stand out.
- A location where authority, money, and gossip overlap.

---

## Location

**Name:** The Gilded Hart Hotel  
**Street:** Hartglass Row, High Quarter, Caer Oryn  
**Nearest landmark:** Three streets east of House Vaelisar, above the eastern spur of the Laurel Stair  
**Neighbourhood character:** Wealthy townhouses, private clubs, legal chambers, discreet shrines, coach houses, and gated residences  
**Clientele:** Minor nobility, Directorate clerks, rich merchants, foreign envoys, retired officers, temple dignitaries, discreet lovers, and high-paid agents who know how to behave in public  
**Reputation:** Old, expensive, respectable, and quietly useful

Hartglass Row is a sloped, gaslit street paved in pale stone veined with green-grey mineral lines. It runs between the older manor houses of the High Quarter and a crescent of newer civic buildings. The street has wrought-iron balconies, polished brass lamps, clipped bay trees in stone planters, private carriage alcoves, and watch-posts that look ornamental until someone tries to run.

House Vaelisar is not visible from the main doors, but its roofline can be seen from the upper east-facing rooms and from the hotel’s rear winter terrace. From the third floor, a character can see the upper chimneys and the slate crown of the Vaelisar manor through a break between two neighbouring residences.

Since the Civic Directorate sealed House Vaelisar, Hartglass Row has become busier than usual. Nobody says this openly. Coaches linger too long. Servants cross the street to speak in doorways. Watchmen appear in pairs rather than alone. A chalk-white Directorate notice-board has been fixed to the corner outside the nearby solicitors’ hall, warning against obstruction, trespass, and rumour-mongering relating to a sealed property.

The Gilded Hart benefits from the increased traffic. It is full of people pretending they are not here because of House Vaelisar.

---

## Exterior

The Gilded Hart occupies a broad corner building of cream stone, dark timber, and green copper roofing. Its sign is an elegant gold-leaf hart with branching antlers, one hoof raised over a small crescent of black enamel. The sign is tasteful, old, and expensive. It creaks softly in the wind.

The main entrance faces Hartglass Row. A smaller guest entrance opens onto Lantern Mews at the rear, used by private visitors, late arrivals, and people who do not want their carriage seen at the front.

### Exterior Details

- Two liveried doormen stand beneath a brass-and-glass canopy from late afternoon until after midnight.
- The front steps are swept constantly, even in bad weather.
- A discreet iron grille covers each basement window.
- A private coach arch leads to an inner courtyard with stables and servant access.
- A hotel messenger boy is almost always outside, waiting for errands.
- The building smells faintly of beeswax, rain on stone, pipe tobacco, roasting fowl, and expensive perfume.

### First Impression

Read or paraphrase:

> The Gilded Hart sits on the corner of Hartglass Row like a building that has never needed to raise its voice. Warm light glows behind tall windows. Carriages roll up beneath a brass canopy. Liveried staff move quickly, quietly, and with the confidence of people who know every name that matters. Inside, music and conversation spill through the doors in controlled bursts before the doormen close the noise away again.

## Surrounding Environment

### Hartglass Row

Hartglass Row is not openly dangerous, but it is heavily watched. Most threats here wear gloves and carry documents.

During the day, the street is full of:

- Clerks walking between civic offices and private townhouses.
- Solicitors and advocates in dark coats.
- Couriers carrying sealed leather document tubes.
- House servants shopping for wine, candles, and imported fruit.
- Temple representatives calling on patrons.
- Watch patrols pretending to be relaxed.

At night, the street changes. The lamps are bright, the carriages more discreet, and the conversations lower. The hotel becomes one of the few places nearby where respectable people can gather without admitting that they are gathering.

### Nearby Buildings

**Veyne & Caldris, Advocates of Record**  
A legal office two doors down. Its windows are shuttered after sunset, but senior clerks have been coming and going late since House Vaelisar was sealed.

**The Old Laurel Counting Rooms**  
A private financial house across the road. Its guards are better armed than city watchmen. A good place for the party to notice that moneyed interests are paying attention.

**Saint Oravel’s Quiet House**  
A small, polished shrine used by wealthy families for private rites, mourning, and reputation management. Clergy here hear plenty but say little.

**Lantern Mews**  
The rear lane behind the hotel. Clean by Caer Oryn standards, but narrower and more useful for movement. Servants, grooms, discreet guests, and hotel staff use it constantly. At night it has two lamps, one of which is usually failing.

**House Vaelisar**  
Three streets west. The direct route from the Gilded Hart passes a small civic barrier and two watch-points. The indirect route via Lantern Mews, Briarwrit Lane, and the upper service steps avoids the main notice-board but passes more private guards.

## The Civic Directorate Presence

The Directorate has not taken over the hotel, but they have made themselves present.

Current signs of pressure:

- Two Civic Directorate officers take breakfast in the hotel most mornings.
- A sealed notice has been delivered to the proprietor warning against harbouring anyone suspected of interfering with a civic closure.
- A clerk has asked to inspect the guest register twice in the last day.
- Watch patrols pass the front every half hour after sunset.
- A plain-clothed observer sometimes sits in the public lounge reading the same page of a broadsheet for too long.

The hotel staff do not like this. Not because they are loyal to House Vaelisar, but because Directorate attention is bad for business.

## Blending In

The Gilded Hart is not built for anonymity. It is built for status.

A character blends in more easily if they:

- Wear clean formal clothing or robes appropriate to station.
- Speak confidently but not loudly.
- Tip staff without making a display of it.
- Have a credible reason to be in the High Quarter.
- Use titles, temple authority, legal business, foreign envoy status, or money as cover.

A character stands out if they:

- Are visibly armed beyond a dress blade, staff, or ceremonial weapon.
- Are muddy, bloodied, openly poor, or carrying adventuring gear.
- Are non-human in a way the High Quarter considers unusual.
- Ask direct questions about House Vaelisar without social cover.
- Try to intimidate staff or guests in public.

### Specific Party Pressure

**Nakoa or any visibly exotic non-human character**  
A tabaxi will be noticed immediately. Not attacked, not refused, but watched. The High Quarter likes foreign curiosities when they are presented as guests, envoys, retainers, performers, or bodyguards. It distrusts them when they appear independent and armed.

**Teclis**  
Teclis can use refinement, elven bearing, and religious connection as social cover, but his authority will not override a Civic Directorate seal. Staff may treat him respectfully while still refusing to help him cross legal boundaries.

**Cassian**  
Cassian reads as disciplined and potentially respectable, especially if he carries himself like a sworn knight or temple man. The problem is that disciplined armed men are exactly the sort of people the Directorate watches during a sealed-property incident.

### Social Checks

Use these as guidance, not fixed rules.

- **DC 10 Persuasion or Deception:** Book rooms without creating suspicion, provided the party can pay.
- **DC 13 Persuasion:** Get staff to speak casually about unusual traffic near House Vaelisar.
- **DC 15 Deception:** Convince the desk that the party has legitimate High Quarter business.
- **DC 15 Insight:** Notice that one lounge guest is watching who asks about Vaelisar.
- **DC 16 Stealth:** Move through Lantern Mews without being seen by staff or late guests.
- **DC 17 Persuasion:** Get access to an upper room with a partial view toward House Vaelisar when the hotel is already busy.
- **DC 18 Deception or Performance:** Pass as guests of a private patron or minor diplomatic party.
- **DC 20 Persuasion:** Persuade staff to warn the party before Directorate clerks inspect the register again.

Failure should create attention rather than immediate combat. The High Quarter punishes people through records, witnesses, delay, and polite obstruction.

## Interior Overview

The Gilded Hart has four public levels, a cellar level, and a rear courtyard.

### Ground Floor

- Front hall and reception desk.
- Public taproom, polished and lively rather than rough.
- Dining room for guests and well-dressed visitors.
- Card room.
- Blue parlour for private meetings.
- Rear winter terrace overlooking Lantern Mews.
- Service corridors and kitchen access.

### First Floor

- Better guest rooms.
- The long gallery overlooking Hartglass Row.
- Two private dining rooms.
- The Green Salon, used for music, wine, and quiet bargaining.

### Second Floor

- Superior rooms and small suites.
- Rooms with the best street views.
- Two rooms with partial sightlines toward House Vaelisar.

### Third Floor

- Expensive suites beneath the roofline.
- Long-stay apartments for patrons with money or influence.
- The east-facing attic study, not advertised, used by the proprietor for awkward guests or discreet observation.

### Cellars

- Wine stores.
- Beer racks.
- Locked silver room.
- Laundry access.
- Staff stair.
- Old stone passage now bricked and sealed. Staff say it once connected to neighbouring service cellars.

## Atmosphere

The Gilded Hart should feel alive.

There is always noise, but controlled noise: glassware, cutlery, low laughter, chair legs on polished floorboards, a singer in the salon, rain against tall windows, servants moving through side doors, and the occasional burst of laughter from the card room.

The hotel does not smell like a common inn. It smells of wax, wine, citrus peel, warm pastry, old wood, pressed linen, roasting meat, coal smoke, and wet wool drying near a back hearth.

The staff are polite without being soft. They are used to drunk magistrates, foreign merchants, vain nobles, young officers, and guests who think money makes them invisible. They know how to smile while deciding whether someone is a problem.

### Payment Expectations

Guests are expected to pay one night in advance. Unknown guests may be asked for two nights, especially if they arrive armed, dirty, or without introductions.

The proprietor accepts coin, letters of credit, temple-backed notes, and reputable house marks. Bad coin is remembered. So are guests who argue over prices.

## Rooms

### Modest Single Room

Small, clean, and better than most inns elsewhere in the city. Contains a narrow bed, small writing shelf, wash basin, pitcher, wool rug, and a lockable chest. The window usually faces an inner wall or service court.

Good for a character who wants privacy but not comfort. Bad for watching the street.

### Standard Guest Room

A comfortable room with polished floorboards, a broad bed, writing desk, brass lamp, wardrobe, basin, chamber pot cabinet, and a small fireplace. The bedding is white linen with green trim.

The lock is decent. The windows latch from inside. Staff have keys.

### Superior Room

Larger and warmer. Includes a proper desk, upholstered chair, private washstand, mirror, good hearth, and either a street or courtyard view. The walls are papered in pale green and gold.

This is the most suitable normal room for adventurers who want to look like they belong.

### Two-Room Suite

A bedroom and separate sitting room with a writing table, wine cabinet, better fireplace, and enough space to meet visitors privately. Servants can bring food through the side service passage without the guest going downstairs.

Good for planning, interrogation, private healing, or meeting NPCs.

### The Hartglass Suite

The hotel’s best advertised suite. It has a front-facing balcony over Hartglass Row, carved bedposts, a private dining table, heavy curtains, polished sideboard, and a green marble hearth. The balcony is watched from the street but useful for reading the mood outside.

Guests staying here are assumed to be important. That assumption is useful and dangerous.

### Eastward Superior Rooms

Rooms 23 and 24 on the second floor have a broken partial sightline between neighbouring rooftops toward House Vaelisar. The view is not perfect. Characters can see the roofline, upper chimneys, and part of the western wall when the weather is clear.

These rooms are currently in demand because too many people are pretending not to care about House Vaelisar.

Room 24 is occupied at the start by a silk factor named Mellor Vane unless the DM wants it available. Room 23 may be booked with the right pressure, payment, or lie.

### The East Attic Study

Not listed on the room board. It is a narrow, angled room under the roof with a small round window that gives the best view toward the Vaelisar roofline. It is dusty but dry. The proprietor uses it for old ledgers and guests who need to be hidden from the public eye for a few hours.

Access requires the proprietor’s permission, theft of a key, or careful movement through the staff stair.

## Food and Drink

The Gilded Hart serves food appropriate to wealthy Caer Oryn.

Common offerings:

- Hot white rolls with salt butter.
- Roasted river trout with lemon and herbs.
- Braised beef in dark ale.
- Pheasant pie with mushrooms.
- Onion soup with melted cheese.
- Spiced pear tart.
- Hard cheeses, pickled walnuts, and smoked meats.
- Strong coffee for clerks and late-night guests.
- Mulled wine in winter or wet weather.
- Clear herb spirits served in small glasses.

The house drink is **Hartglass Gold**, a bright fortified wine served chilled in small measures. It is too sweet for some tastes but popular among young officers and visiting merchants.

## House Rules

The rules are written on a polished brass plaque beside the desk.

1. No drawn steel in the public rooms.
2. No spellcasting in the public rooms without permission.
3. No unpaid guests above the first floor.
4. Servants are not to be threatened, propositioned, or followed.
5. Debts are settled before departure.
6. The proprietor reserves the right to refuse service without explanation.

The actual unwritten rules matter more:

- Do not embarrass the hotel.
- Do not bring the Directorate through the front door unless they were already coming.
- Do not make staff choose between loyalty and survival.
- Do not assume privacy just because the room is expensive.

## Security

The Gilded Hart uses quiet security rather than obvious force.

### Visible Security

**Two doormen** at the front after afternoon bell. Both are large, polite, and used to removing drunk aristocrats without making it look like violence.

**One courtyard guard** near the coach arch after sunset.

**House locks** are good quality. A competent thief can beat them, but not casually.

### Hidden Security

- Staff watch the stairs.
- The night porter records late movement.
- The proprietor keeps a small black book of difficult guests.
- The card room has a peephole from the service corridor.
- A bell cord behind reception alerts the kitchen, courtyard guard, and night porter.
- The silver room in the cellar has a reinforced door and a concealed latch.

### Weapons Policy

Dress blades, walking sticks, ceremonial weapons, and obvious holy implements are tolerated. Crossbows, polearms, heavy shields, bloodied blades, and battlefield kit cause problems.

Guests may be asked to leave heavier weapons in a locked cloakroom. Refusing is not illegal, but it will draw attention.

### Magic Policy

Minor convenience magic is tolerated if subtle. Visible spellcasting in the dining room causes silence, then staff intervention. The hotel has no anti-magic wards, but it does have social consequences.

A character using Detect Magic notices minor protective charms worked into the reception desk, the proprietor’s office door, and the silver room. These are alarm charms, not combat wards.

## Important NPCs

## Marcelline Orras

**Role:** Proprietor of the Gilded Hart  
**Ancestry:** Human  
**Age:** Late 50s  
**Appearance:** Tall, immaculate, silver-streaked black hair pinned hard against the skull, dark green dress-coat, pearl earrings, severe eyes  
**Voice:** Quiet, exact, never rushed  
**Public face:** Hospitable, controlled, professionally warm  
**Private truth:** She knows the High Quarter survives on favours, silence, and timing

Marcelline inherited the Gilded Hart from her mother and has run it for nearly thirty years. She has hosted judges, priests, officers, lovers, envoys, liars, and fugitives. She is not easily intimidated.

She dislikes the Civic Directorate but respects its reach. She will not risk the hotel for strangers unless the party gives her a strong reason, serious payment, or leverage.

### What Marcelline Knows

- House Vaelisar was sealed during the night, not in the morning as the official notice implies.
- The first Directorate carriage arrived without lamps.
- At least one Vaelisar servant tried to leave through the lower service road before dawn and was turned back.
- The Directorate clerk who inspected her register was nervous and had ink on his cuff, suggesting he had been writing notices in a hurry.
- Somebody important wants the closure treated as a legal matter, not a scandal.
- The Directorate has asked after foreign guests, religious agents, and armed travellers.

### What She Will Say Freely

"House Vaelisar is under civic seal. That is all anyone sensible needs to know. Sensible people sleep better. They also live longer."

"My staff do not gossip. They observe. There is a difference."

"If you intend to make trouble, do not do it from my front steps. I have spent too many years polishing them."

### What She Might Give the Party

- A room that faces the correct direction, if they pay and behave.
- A warning if Directorate clerks request the register again.
- Access to the East Attic Study for one hour.
- A servant’s description of the Vaelisar service route.
- The name of a coachman who drove near the manor during the night.

### Line in the Sand

Marcelline will not knowingly help the party break into House Vaelisar unless she believes the Directorate is about to endanger the whole street or the party proves the matter is larger than family scandal.

## Odran Vale

**Role:** Head porter and quiet security  
**Ancestry:** Human  
**Age:** Early 40s  
**Appearance:** Broad shoulders, broken nose, close-cropped hair, black porter’s coat, white gloves that never quite hide scarred knuckles  
**Voice:** Flat, practical, dry  
**Public face:** Polite muscle  
**Private truth:** Former watch sergeant who left after refusing to bury a bad arrest

Odran manages doors, bags, drunks, and problems. He knows how to read weapons and posture. He will clock adventurers almost immediately.

He is not corrupt, but he understands survival. He can be persuaded by competence, honesty, and respect for staff. He hates bullies.

### What Odran Knows

- The watch patrols on Hartglass Row are heavier than normal and not all of them are regular High Quarter watchmen.
- One of the men posted near House Vaelisar walks like a soldier, not a watchman.
- A pale coach with covered lamps passed through Lantern Mews close to third bell after midnight.
- A young Vaelisar maid named Resha used to visit the hotel kitchen door for leftover bread and gossip.
- Resha has not been seen since the sealing.
- A man in a Directorate cloak asked if the hotel had taken in any "religious investigators".

### What He Says

"You people carry yourselves like you expect doors to become problems. Around here, doors have paperwork behind them. Worse than locks."

"If you start a fight in the taproom, I will put you outside. If you start a fight with the Directorate, I will pretend I never saw you. That is not cowardice. That is mathematics."

"The Vaelisar place was quiet too quickly. Houses do not go quiet like that unless people are told to stop making noise."

### Useful Behaviour

Odran may quietly help the party if they protect staff, tip fairly, and do not treat him like hired muscle. He can describe patrol rhythms around Hartglass Row.

## Selda Morn

**Role:** Night clerk  
**Ancestry:** Half-elf or human with elven ancestry, depending on what best fits the table  
**Age:** Appears early 30s  
**Appearance:** Pale brown skin, tired eyes, ink-stained fingers, neat grey waistcoat, spectacles on a chain  
**Voice:** Soft, quick, slightly nasal when nervous  
**Public face:** Efficient, apologetic, forgettable  
**Private truth:** She remembers every guest and lies better than she looks

Selda works the desk from evening until dawn. She sees who comes in late, who leaves early, and who pretends not to know one another.

She is cautious but curious. She dislikes the Directorate because they treat clerks like furniture. A respectful character can get more from her than from almost anyone else.

### What Selda Knows

- A Directorate clerk named Halven Dray inspected the register and copied names of recent arrivals.
- Dray specifically noted guests from outside Caer Oryn.
- Two rooms were booked under false names this week. One guest paid in old Havenor coin.
- Room 24 has been held by Mellor Vane, a silk factor who barely leaves the window.
- A woman in mourning black asked whether House Vaelisar had any living nephews, then left without staying.
- The hotel received a letter warning them not to accept sealed Vaelisar property for safekeeping.

### What She Says

"People think night clerks are half asleep. That is why they say things in front of us."

"The Directorate man copied names as if he was afraid of missing one. Not bored. Afraid. There is a difference."

"If you want a room with a view, half the street already had the same thought. Nobody will admit why."

### Possible Help

Selda can quietly delay the register, misfile a note, or send a warning to the party’s room. She will not do this if threatened.

## Iven Tallow

**Role:** Senior waiter and taproom listener  
**Ancestry:** Human  
**Age:** Mid 60s  
**Appearance:** Thin, bald crown, sharp beard, immaculate white apron, one cloudy eye  
**Voice:** Warm, amused, faintly mocking  
**Public face:** Charming old servant  
**Private truth:** Knows exactly which powerful people are fools

Iven has worked at the Gilded Hart since before Marcelline owned it. He serves wine, remembers scandals, and knows how to make a guest comfortable enough to talk.

He is useful for rumours and social mapping. He rarely knows the full truth, but he knows who is lying about what.

### What Iven Knows

- Lord Vaelisar rarely entertained at the hotel, but his household bought wine through the Gilded Hart’s supplier.
- Vaelisar orders changed two weeks ago: less dinner wine, more strong spirits, more lamp oil.
- A junior advocate from Veyne & Caldris got drunk and said, "They cannot seal blood with wax."
- The phrase "inventory discrepancy" has been used by nervous clerks discussing the Vaelisar matter.
- Several guests have asked whether the Vaelisar cellars connect to older High Quarter service tunnels.
- Iven thinks the answer is yes, but he has no proof.

### What He Says

"Wine tells you more than people. A house that orders less claret and more lamp oil is either economising or afraid of the dark. The Vaelisars were not economising."

"The High Quarter does not have secrets. It has invoices. Find the invoice and you find the sin."

"A man who says 'inventory discrepancy' over breakfast has either lost a crate or found a corpse."

## Brienne Cask

**Role:** Cook and kitchen authority  
**Ancestry:** Dwarf  
**Age:** 118  
**Appearance:** Square build, red-brown hair wrapped in a scarf, flour on her sleeves, burn scars on both forearms  
**Voice:** Direct, loud in the kitchen, quiet when serious  
**Public face:** Not seen by most guests  
**Private truth:** Runs the staff better than Marcelline runs the front

Brienne controls the kitchen, the servants’ gossip, and the rear door. She does not care about noble manners. She cares about whether people eat, work, and keep their hands to themselves.

She can be a strong ally if the party treats the working staff well.

### What Brienne Knows

- Vaelisar servants sometimes came to the hotel kitchen door because Brienne fed them scraps and listened.
- Resha, a Vaelisar maid, said the house had become "cold in the middle" even when fires were lit.
- One Vaelisar footman had purple-black bruising on his wrist and claimed it came from carrying coal.
- The Vaelisar household stopped sending laundry out three days before the seal.
- A kitchen girl saw two men carrying a covered chest through Lantern Mews before dawn.
- The men were not hotel staff and not normal watch.

### What She Says

"Nobles go strange in the head all the time. This was not that. Servants know the difference."

"That girl Resha laughed like a kettle. You could hear her from the mews. Haven't heard her since. That tells me enough."

"If you want to know what happened in a great house, ask who stopped sending out sheets. Blood, sickness, shame, or all three."

## Pell Vask

**Role:** Messenger boy and runner  
**Ancestry:** Human  
**Age:** 13  
**Appearance:** Thin, quick, missing front tooth, oversized hotel cap, constantly damp shoes  
**Voice:** Fast, cheeky until frightened  
**Public face:** Errand boy  
**Private truth:** Sees more of Hartglass Row than anyone with a title

Pell runs messages, fetches parcels, watches carriages, and knows which gates are easier to slip through. He is brave in the way children are brave when they do not fully understand consequences.

### What Pell Knows

- A pale coach stopped near Lantern Mews after midnight and one wheel squealed badly.
- The same squeal was heard again near the solicitor’s hall before dawn.
- A Directorate man paid Pell not to loiter near the Vaelisar corner.
- Pell loitered anyway from farther away.
- He saw a servant or prisoner at an upper Vaelisar window for a moment, then the shutters closed.
- He thinks one of the watchmen has a burn on his neck shaped like a narrow loop or tear, but he only saw it briefly.

### What He Says

"I weren't spying. I was walking slow. Different thing."

"Rich folk always think children vanish when they stop looking at them. We don't. We just get lower down."

"The coach had a squeak like a dying gull. Heard it twice. Same wheel. Same night."

### Risk

Pell should not be used as disposable bait. If the party sends him too close to danger, he may be caught, followed, or frightened into silence.

## Ser Jorren Pike

**Role:** Regular guest, retired officer  
**Ancestry:** Human  
**Age:** Early 70s  
**Appearance:** White moustache, stiff posture, left hand tremor, old cavalry pin on his coat  
**Voice:** Gruff, clipped, more emotional after wine  
**Public face:** Respectable old soldier  
**Private truth:** Hates seeing soldiers used as civic thugs

Jorren drinks in the same chair most evenings. He is difficult, proud, and observant. He notices gait, discipline, and chain of command.

### What Jorren Knows

- Some men around House Vaelisar are not city watch. Their boots and spacing are wrong.
- The one commanding them is trying to look like a clerk but holds himself like an officer.
- The Directorate seal was applied in haste, probably after the building was already secured.
- A proper quarantine would have brought physicians. He has seen no physicians.
- A proper legal seizure would have brought advocates. He has seen too few advocates.

### What He Says

"Those are not watchmen. Some are, yes. Not all. Watchmen slouch. Soldiers pretend not to."

"A sealed house without doctors, priests, or shouting relatives is not a scandal. It is an operation."

"If you are going to sneak, do not sneak like amateurs. The High Quarter has curtains, and behind every curtain is a bored old bastard like me."

## Mellor Vane

**Role:** Silk factor occupying Room 24  
**Ancestry:** Human  
**Age:** Late 30s  
**Appearance:** Soft hands, narrow beard, expensive red-brown coat, perfume too heavy  
**Voice:** Smooth until cornered  
**Public face:** Merchant waiting on a delayed contract  
**Private truth:** Paid to watch House Vaelisar from the hotel window

Mellor claims to be in Caer Oryn for textile negotiations. That is partly true. He is also being paid by an unknown patron to record movement around House Vaelisar. He is not brave. He is not a mastermind. He is a paid watcher who now realises the matter may be more dangerous than he expected.

### What Mellor Knows

- Room 24 has a partial view of the Vaelisar roofline and west wall.
- He was paid in advance by a woman wearing mourning black and a veil.
- His instructions were to record lights, smoke, opened shutters, and anyone leaving after midnight.
- He saw upper windows go dark one by one shortly before the seal was posted.
- He saw a brief black-purple pulse of light behind one upper shutter, then nothing.
- He has written notes but keeps them sewn into the lining of his travelling case.

### What He Says If Pressured

"I was paid to look. Looking is not a crime. Not unless the Directorate has changed that too."

"There was a light. Not candlelight. Not lightning. I know the difference. I sell silk; I know colour."

"The woman paid me with old coin and told me not to ask her name. So I did not ask. I am not heroic. I am alive."

### Use at the Table

Mellor is an excellent source of information if the party intimidates him, bribes him, charms him, or steals his notes. He may also report them if he thinks they are more dangerous than his patron.

## Directorate Observer: Alren Dusk

**Role:** Plain-clothed Civic Directorate observer  
**Ancestry:** Human  
**Age:** Early 30s  
**Appearance:** Brown coat, neat gloves, ordinary face, eyes that keep returning to the stairs  
**Voice:** Mild, dull, bureaucratic  
**Public face:** Clerk reading broadsheets  
**Private truth:** Intelligence asset watching who asks about Vaelisar

Alren is not staying at the hotel. He appears in the lounge for one or two hours at a time, usually late morning or early evening. He orders tea and reads the same broadsheet. He watches the front desk, the stairs, and anyone who asks the wrong questions.

### What Alren Knows

- He has been told to record names, appearances, and associates of anyone asking about House Vaelisar.
- He knows the Directorate seal is covering something beyond ordinary legal seizure.
- He does not know the full truth.
- He has heard the phrase "containment concern".
- He has been warned to avoid direct confrontation with religious authorities unless they cross the seal.
- He is especially interested in outsiders, armed guests, and anyone with arcane or temple credentials.

### What He Says If Confronted

"I am having tea. That remains legal, I believe."

"If you have business with the Directorate, submit it properly. If you do not, I advise developing less interesting habits."

"House Vaelisar is closed under civic authority. Curiosity does not constitute standing."

### Behaviour

Alren leaves rather than fights unless cornered. If threatened, he reports the party. If followed, he leads them through a dull route toward a civic annex where two uniformed officers are waiting.

## Possible Scenes

### Checking In Under Watch

The party arrives while Alren Dusk is seated in the lounge. Selda or Marcelline asks for names. Odran watches their weapons. A well-dressed couple pauses mid-conversation when Nakoa enters. Nobody is openly hostile, but everyone notices.

Pressure points:

- Do they give real names?
- Do they declare weapons?
- Does Teclis use religious or elven authority?
- Does Cassian look like protection, trouble, or both?
- Does Nakoa lean into being exotic, bodyguard, envoy, or shadow?

### Dinner Near the Wrong People

The party eats in the dining room near Ser Jorren Pike, two Directorate clerks, and a nervous advocate from Veyne & Caldris. The conversation around them creates openings.

Useful overheard lines:

- "The seal was not dry when they wrote the notice."
- "No, not plague. If it were plague, Oravel’s people would be there."
- "Vaelisar always paid in advance. Always."
- "I heard they barred the servants inside. That cannot be legal."

### The Window Room

Room 23 is available only if the party pays extra or convinces Selda to move another guest. From the window, they can observe faint details of the Vaelisar upper house.

Possible sightings:

- A shutter opening and closing quickly.
- A Directorate officer smoking beneath an arch.
- A servant’s pale face at a high window.
- A momentary dim violet-black pulse after midnight.
- A covered object carried through the service gate.

### Mellor Gets Nervous

Mellor Vane realises the party are also interested in House Vaelisar. He may try to buy their silence, leave suddenly, or report them to his unknown patron. If searched, his travelling case contains stitched notes.

Example note fragments:

- "Second upper east shutter closed before bell. No candle after."
- "Three men, not watch. One limping. One with wrapped neck."
- "Violet-black light behind glass. Duration shorter than breath."
- "No servants seen leaving. No laundry. No refuse carts."

### Directorate Register Check

Halven Dray or another Directorate clerk arrives to inspect the register again. Selda may warn the party first if they have earned trust.

Complications:

- The party used false names.
- Their room has a Vaelisar sightline.
- They have been asking questions.
- A staff member has been bribed.
- Alren Dusk is present and paying attention.

### The Kitchen Door

Brienne Cask allows the party into the kitchen if they need a less public conversation. The kitchen is hot, loud, and full of staff who pretend not to listen.

Brienne can tell them about Resha, the stopped laundry, and the covered chest. She will demand plain speaking in return.

## The Hotel as a Base

The Gilded Hart can support different approaches.

### Social Approach

The party uses dinners, staff tips, temple respectability, and controlled questions to gather information. This keeps them safer but slower.

### Surveillance Approach

The party books Room 23, pressures Mellor, or gains access to the East Attic Study. This gives useful sightings but risks being noticed by Alren or Directorate clerks.

### Servant Route Approach

The party befriends Brienne, Pell, or Odran to learn the movement of service lanes behind Hartglass Row. This may lead toward Lantern Mews and possible routes near House Vaelisar.

### Confrontational Approach

The party pushes too hard, threatens staff, or challenges Directorate authority in public. This causes records, attention, and obstruction before it causes combat.

## Routes from the Gilded Hart to House Vaelisar

### Direct Route

**Hartglass Row -> Laurel Stair Spur -> Vaelisar Corner**

Fastest and most visible. Watch patrols are common. A Civic Directorate notice-board stands near the corner.

Use when the party wants to test official pressure or arrive openly.

### Discreet Route

**Lantern Mews -> Briarwrit Lane -> Upper Service Steps -> Rear wall district**

Less formal, but not empty. Servants, grooms, private guards, and late-night messengers use these lanes.

Use when the party wants movement without crossing the main civic barrier.

### Roofline Route

Possible but risky. The High Quarter roofs are steep, wet, and overlooked by too many windows. A tabaxi or agile rogue might manage it, but a full party will draw attention.

Use only if the party accepts that being seen on rooftops in the High Quarter is suspicious by default.

### Cellar Rumour Route

The hotel cellars do not lead directly to House Vaelisar. However, old service infrastructure beneath Hartglass Row may connect several properties through bricked or sealed passages. The Gilded Hart has one sealed arch in its lower cellar wall.

Opening it would be loud, illegal, and difficult without tools or magic. It may lead only to an old coal run, collapsed passage, or neighbouring cellar. It should not be a clean shortcut unless the DM wants it to be.

## Secrets for the DM

- Marcelline has hidden guests before, but never from the Civic Directorate during an active seal.
- Odran suspects the Vaelisar operation includes non-watch personnel but cannot prove it.
- Selda copied one page of the register before the Directorate clerk took notes. She can compare whether names were altered later.
- Brienne knows Resha the Vaelisar maid and is personally worried about her.
- Pell saw more than he understands. The mark on the man’s neck may connect to the underground intelligence faction.
- Mellor Vane’s unknown patron may be trying to identify Vaelisar heirs, track the artifact, or confirm whether the black-purple phenomenon has manifested.
- Alren Dusk is not the main threat. He is a low-level watcher. Hurting him brings more heat than value.
- Room 23 and the East Attic Study can both give the party a reason to witness strange activity from House Vaelisar before they infiltrate.

## Tone Notes

The Gilded Hart should not feel like a cosy adventurer inn. It should feel like a polished institution where everyone is polite because politeness is armour.

The danger is not that the staff will stab the party. The danger is that the wrong name appears in the wrong book, a clerk asks the wrong question, or an observant guest remembers that an armed tabaxi, an elven religious agent, and a disciplined human warrior all arrived two streets from a sealed manor on the same night.
